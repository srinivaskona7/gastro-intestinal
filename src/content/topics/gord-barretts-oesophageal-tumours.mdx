## What it is

**Gastro-oesophageal reflux disease (GORD)** is the backflow of stomach contents into the oesophagus. Long-standing reflux can change the lining (**Barrett's oesophagus**) and may lead to cancer. Oesophageal tumours come in two main types that differ in cause and location.

<Anatomy figure="oesophagus" highlight={['lower-oesophageal-sphincter', 'gastro-oesophageal-junction']} caption="Oesophagus: lower oesophageal sphincter, gastro oesophageal junction" />

## Causes and risk factors

The underlying problem is a **defective lower oesophageal sphincter**. Factors that promote reflux:

- Raised intra-abdominal pressure
- Hiatus hernia
- Fatty foods, alcohol, coffee, chocolate and spices

## Pathogenesis and morphology

Repeated acid exposure damages the squamous lining, which is replaced by gastric-type columnar epithelium (metaplasia). Cancer can then arise from this.

| Condition | Endoscopic appearance | Location and cause |
|---|---|---|
| Barrett's oesophagus | Squamo-columnar junction moves upwards; darker pink, velvety, inflamed mucosa with an irregular border | Lower oesophagus, from GORD |
| Adenocarcinoma | Wet, bulbous tumour surrounded by gastric metaplasia (Barrett's) | Lower third; GORD and Barrett's |
| Squamous cell carcinoma | Dry, keratinised, ulcerative tumour | Upper third; smoking and alcohol |

## Clinical features

- **Heartburn**: a retrosternal burning sensation.
- **Dysphagia**: difficulty swallowing, usually solids first and then liquids.

Complications of reflux disease: bleeding, perforation, stricture or obstruction, and Barrett's oesophagus.

**Malignancy** adds progressive dysphagia (solids then liquids), weight loss, anorexia and aspiration.

<Callout kind="warning" title="Progressive dysphagia">
Dysphagia that worsens from solids to liquids, with weight loss, is a red flag for oesophageal cancer and needs urgent endoscopy.
</Callout>

## Investigations

- **Gastroscopy with biopsy** to grade changes from Barrett's metaplasia up to adenocarcinoma.
- **CT** to stage adenocarcinoma.

## Management

1. **Conservative (lifestyle)**: raise the head of the bed; avoid meals within 3 hours of bedtime; cut down fat, alcohol, caffeine, spices and smoking; lose weight to reduce intra-abdominal pressure.
2. **Medical**: antacids (Mylanta), alginates (aluminium salts), H2 histamine receptor antagonists (ranitidine) and proton-pump inhibitors (omeprazole).
3. **Surgical**: **Nissen fundoplication**, wrapping the upper stomach around the oesophagus.

<Callout kind="pearl">
Lower third, wet and bulbous, Barrett's nearby: adenocarcinoma. Upper third, dry and keratinised: squamous, linked to smoking and alcohol.
</Callout>
