## Overview

| Disease | Core mechanism | Key test | Main treatment |
|---|---|---|---|
| Autoimmune hepatitis | Immune attack on hepatocytes | ALT and AST very high, ANA, anti-liver antibodies | Prednisolone plus azathioprine |
| Primary biliary cholangitis | Autoimmune destruction of small bile ducts | ALP, GGT, bilirubin high; AMA | Ursodeoxycholic acid, transplant |
| Gilbert syndrome | Reduced bilirubin conjugation | Isolated unconjugated bilirubin rise | None |
| Haemochromatosis | Iron overload | Ferritin, transferrin saturation, HFE gene | Venesection |

## Autoimmune hepatitis

**Cause:** an autoimmune response, often found alongside other autoimmune conditions such as pernicious anaemia, thyroiditis, coeliac disease and autoimmune haemolytic anaemia.

<Mermaid
  caption="Autoimmune hepatitis course"
  chart={`flowchart LR
  A[Autoimmune response] --> B[Inflammation]
  B --> C[Progressive necrosis]
  C --> D[Fibrosis]
  D --> E[Cirrhosis]`}
/>

- **Symptoms:** may be asymptomatic and found incidentally; otherwise fatigue, fever, polyarthritis, pleurisy.
- **Signs:** jaundice, and signs of cirrhosis such as striae, ascites and acne.
- **Tests:** markedly raised ALT and AST; ANA positive; anti-liver antibodies.
- **Treatment:** corticosteroids (prednisolone) and azathioprine, an immunosuppressive drug (sometimes loosely called chemotherapy). Liver transplant if treatment fails.
- **Prognosis:** about 80% remission with lifelong steroids plus azathioprine.

## Primary biliary cholangitis (PBC)

Also known as chronic non-suppurative destructive cholangitis. The standard name is primary biliary cholangitis (formerly cirrhosis).

<Callout kind="pearl">
Primary means autoimmune. Secondary biliary disease is due to chronic biliary obstruction.
</Callout>

- **Cause:** autoimmune; specifics unknown. AMA and ANA are present but the exact pathogenesis is unclear.
- **Pathogenesis:** chronic, progressive destruction of intrahepatic bile ducts leading to cirrhosis and then liver failure.
- **Features:** insidious onset, then cholestatic jaundice, fatigue and hepatomegaly.
- **Tests:** raised bilirubin, ALP and GGT; autoantibodies (ANA and AMA positive).
- **Treatment:** supportive measures, ursodeoxycholic acid (improves bilirubin and AST and ALT), corticosteroids (prednisone), and fat-soluble vitamin supplements (A, D, E, K). Liver transplant is definitive.

## Gilbert syndrome

A familial, genetic and **benign** condition. The enzyme for bilirubin glucuronidation (conjugation) is deficient, so conjugation runs at roughly 30% of normal. The result is mild unconjugated hyperbilirubinaemia (sometimes labelled pre-hepatic; strictly it is a hepatic conjugation defect).

| Aspect | Detail |
|---|---|
| Morphology | No visible liver disease |
| Symptoms | Usually none; occasional mild jaundice with fasting, infection, stress or exertion |
| Labs | Raised unconjugated bilirubin; other LFTs normal; normal reticulocyte count, so no haemolysis |
| Treatment | None needed |

<Callout kind="pearl">
Normal reticulocytes and normal other LFTs distinguish Gilbert from haemolysis and liver disease.
</Callout>

## Haemochromatosis

Body iron overload caused by excessive absorption.

| Type | Cause |
|---|---|
| Primary (congenital) | HFE gene mutation |
| Secondary (acquired) | Transfusions, supplements, haemolysis |

<Mermaid
  caption="Iron deposition and its effects"
  chart={`flowchart TD
  A[Excess iron] --> B[Deposited in organs]
  B --> C[Liver: cirrhosis]
  B --> D[Heart: cardiomyopathy]
  B --> E[Endocrine glands: failure, diabetes]
  B --> F[Joints: arthritis]
  B --> G[Skin: bronzing]`}
/>

- **Symptoms:** initially none; early fatigue, arthralgia and loss of libido; later skin bronzing, abdominal pain, hepatomegaly, cirrhosis and heart disease.
- **Long-term effects:** arthritis, liver disease, heart disease, impotence, early menopause, bronze complexion and diabetes.
- **Diagnosis:** iron studies (raised ferritin, raised transferrin saturation), LFTs and HFE gene testing.
- **Treatment:** venesection and a low-iron diet.
