## What it is

**Pseudomembranous colitis** is colonic inflammation in which a false membrane (pseudomembrane) coats the mucosa. It is caused by overgrowth of ***Clostridium difficile***, a bacterium that damages the bowel directly through its toxins.

## Cause and pathogenesis

C. difficile is normally held in check by the **commensal bacteria** of the gut. **Antibiotics** kill those commensals, so C. difficile overgrows and produces enterotoxins, which inflame the colon and produce a fibrous pseudomembrane.

## Morphology

- Mucosal inflammation, ulceration and bleeding.
- **Pseudomembranes**: fibrous sacs full of purulent debris that stick firmly to the mucosa.

## Clinical features

- Onset usually within **2 days** of starting an antibiotic, and it can persist for **2 weeks** after stopping.
- Fever and abdominal cramps.
- **Profuse watery diarrhoea**, under 10 times a day.
- Haematochezia.

## Diagnosis

- **Stool sample** for a C. difficile assay.
- **Colonoscopy** shows the pseudomembranes and also screens for differentials: Crohn's disease, ulcerative colitis and cancer.

## Treatment

1. Stop the causative antibiotic.
2. Anti-clostridial antibiotic: **metronidazole or vancomycin**.
3. Probiotics.
4. Supportive care: rehydration and electrolyte replacement.

<Callout kind="warning">
Severe disease can lead to toxic megacolon and perforation. A distended, tender abdomen with sudden drop in diarrhoea is an emergency.
</Callout>

## Complications

- Dehydration and electrolyte imbalance
- Perforation
- Toxic megacolon
