## What it is

These conditions damage the liver as a consequence of something else: a drug and virus combination, pregnancy, heart failure or a blocked vein.

| Condition | Trigger | Key liver feature |
|---|---|---|
| Reye syndrome | Aspirin plus viral URTI in children | Fatty liver, no jaundice |
| Pre-eclampsia, eclampsia, HELLP | Pregnancy | Hepatocellular necrosis |
| Nutmeg liver | Right heart failure | Zone 3 congestion |
| Budd-Chiari | Hepatic vein occlusion | Congestion plus portal hypertension |

<Anatomy figure="portal-system" highlight={['liver', 'hepatic-veins', 'ivc']} caption="Portal system: liver, hepatic veins, ivc" />

## Reye syndrome

**Cause.** A disease of children aged 4 to 12, linked to **aspirin** taken during a viral upper respiratory infection. The exact mechanism is unknown, but it produces acute encephalopathy and liver failure.

**Morphology.** Fatty liver with hepatomegaly, plus cerebral oedema and encephalopathy.

**Clinical features.** Persistent vomiting, then lethargy, irritability, aggression and confusion, progressing to seizures and coma. There is **no jaundice**.

**Treatment and prognosis.** No specific treatment, supportive care only. Most children recover fully. Complications are severe brain damage, respiratory arrest and multi-organ failure.

## Toxaemia of pregnancy

### Pathogenesis

Placental vasoconstrictors cause **endothelial dysfunction** in blood vessels, leading to hypertension.

### Clinical features

- **Pre-eclampsia**: blood pressure above 140/90, proteinuria, and oedema of the face and hands.
- **Eclampsia**: seizures, with risk of stroke, coma and death.

### Complications

- Poor foetal growth.
- Fulminant hepatic failure from hepatocellular necrosis.
- **HELLP syndrome**: **H**aemolysis, **E**levated **L**iver enzymes, **L**ow **P**latelets, causing jaundice, epigastric pain and vomiting.

### Treatment

Bed rest as supportive care; antihypertensives (labetalol); vasodilators and seizure prophylaxis (magnesium sulfate, nifedipine); antenatal corticosteroids (betamethasone) for foetal lung maturity. **Definitive treatment is delivery**, by early induction of labour.

<Callout kind="pearl">
Magnesium sulfate is mainly used to prevent eclamptic seizures. The only cure for pre-eclampsia is delivering the baby and placenta.
</Callout>

## Nutmeg liver (congestive hepatopathy)

**Cause.** Congestive cardiac failure, specifically right-sided failure.

**Pathogenesis.** Backed-up pressure in the IVC causes venous stasis and chronic passive congestion of the liver. Hypoperfusion causes necrosis and congestion causes haemorrhage.

**Morphology.** Macroscopically a mottled red and pale "nutmeg" cut surface. Microscopically, **centrilobular (zone 3)** congestion with red blood cells and zone 3 necrosis.

**Clinical features.**

- Symptoms: often none, found on routine liver tests; RUQ pain from stretching of the liver capsule.
- Signs: firm, smooth hepatomegaly; ascites; pedal oedema; raised JVP; hepatic encephalopathy; jaundice (unconjugated, from hepatocellular dysfunction, haemolysis and canalicular obstruction).
- Lab: raised AST/ALT.
- Outcome: severe or chronic disease leads to fibrosis and **cardiac cirrhosis**; fulminant failure is rare.

## Budd-Chiari syndrome

**Cause.** Obstruction of venous outflow from the liver, from occlusion of the hepatic vein by hypercoagulability (for example polycythaemia), thrombophilia, leukaemia or a compressive tumour.

**Morphology.** Like nutmeg liver: centrizonal congestion and haemorrhage, then centrizonal fibrosis and cirrhosis. The **caudate lobe is spared** because it has its own blood supply and drainage.

**Clinical features.** Abdominal pain, nausea, vomiting, hepatomegaly, ascites, signs of portal hypertension, and a **negative hepatojugular reflux** (unlike cardiac congestion).

**Treatment.** Treat the underlying cause; liver transplant if chronic or fulminant.

<Callout kind="warning" title="Cardiac versus hepatic vein cause">
Ascites with raised JVP and positive hepatojugular reflux suggests heart failure; ascites with a negative reflux suggests Budd-Chiari.
</Callout>
