## What it is

**Portal hypertension** is raised pressure in the portal vein, which carries blood from the gut and spleen to the liver. When flow through or around the liver is blocked, pressure builds behind the block. The sensible way to learn the causes is by asking where the block sits relative to the liver.

<Anatomy figure="portal-system" highlight={['portal-vein', 'oesophageal-collaterals', 'caput-medusae', 'rectal-collaterals']} caption="Portal system: portal vein, oesophageal collaterals, caput medusae, rectal collaterals" />

## Causes by site

| Site | Causes | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| Pre-hepatic | Portal vein thrombosis or occlusion | Blood backs up into the mesenteric veins |
| Intra-hepatic | Cirrhosis (alcoholic, viral, biliary, congenital), primary biliary cirrhosis, schistosomiasis | Scarred or damaged liver resists flow |
| Post-hepatic | IVC obstruction, thrombosis in hepatic veins, alcoholic central-vein sclerosis without cirrhosis | Blood backs up into the hepatic veins and then the liver |

## Consequences

Back-pressure opens collateral routes and forces fluid out. The signs listed with cirrhosis follow from this: **splenomegaly**, **caput medusae**, **oesophageal varices**, **haemorrhoids** and **ascites**. Ascites is helped by low albumin and by hepatorenal salt and water retention. Encephalopathy occurs because blood bypasses the liver, so ammonia is not cleared.

<Callout kind="pearl">
Budd-Chiari and congestive heart failure are post-hepatic mechanisms; schistosomiasis and cirrhosis are intra-hepatic; portal vein clot is pre-hepatic.
</Callout>
