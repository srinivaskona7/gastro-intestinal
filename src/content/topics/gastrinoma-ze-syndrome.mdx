## What it is

**Zollinger-Ellison (ZE) syndrome** is the clinical picture produced by a **gastrinoma**: a neuroendocrine adenoma that makes gastrin without any of the normal controls. Gastrin is the hormone that tells the stomach's parietal cells to make acid, so an uncontrolled source means the stomach is bathed in acid all the time.

## Where the tumours sit

Gastrin-secreting tumours are found in:

- the **pancreas**
- the **duodenum**
- the **stomach**

## Clinical features

| Feature | Why it happens |
|---|---|
| Abdominal pain and dyspepsia | Acid and pepsin attacking the mucosa |
| Peptic ulcer disease with haematemesis | Ulcers erode a vessel and bleed |
| Ruptured ulcer leading to peritonitis, shock or death | Full-thickness perforation |
| Chronic diarrhoea | Acid inactivates pancreatic enzymes such as lipase, and the damaged mucosa absorbs poorly |

## Diagnosis

- **Fasting serum gastrin** is the biochemical test; it is raised.
- **Endoscopic ultrasound and CT** find the tumour and stage it, since these tumours are often tiny and hard to locate.

## Treatment

- A **proton pump inhibitor** (for example omeprazole) switches off acid production and heals the ulcers.
- **Surgery** removes the gastrinoma. When this is possible, about 80% of patients are alive at five years.

## Complication

The tumour has **malignant potential**, which is why removal, not just acid suppression, is the aim.

<Callout kind="pearl">
Ulcers that are multiple, in unusual sites, or recur despite proper treatment, especially with diarrhoea, should make you think of gastrinoma and check a fasting gastrin.
</Callout>

<Callout kind="warning" title="Diarrhoea here is an acid effect">
The diarrhoea is not from the tumour secreting a toxin. Acid destroys lipase and injures the lining, so fat is poorly digested and absorbed.
</Callout>
