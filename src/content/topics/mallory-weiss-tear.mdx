## What it is

A **Mallory-Weiss tear** is a **longitudinal laceration of the mucosa** where the oesophagus meets the stomach. Only the lining tears, so the wall stays intact and the bleeding is usually minor.

## Why it happens

Anything that suddenly raises intra-abdominal pressure can split the mucosa at the junction:

- Forceful or repeated vomiting and retching (the classic trigger, often after heavy alcohol intake)
- Severe coughing
- Straining or heavy lifting

About three quarters of patients also have a **hiatus hernia**, which leaves the junction less supported.

## What you see

| Feature | Detail |
|---|---|
| Lesion | Single linear mucosal tear at the gastro-oesophageal junction |
| Symptoms | Haematemesis, epigastric or chest pain, sometimes dysphagia |
| Bleeding | Minor upper GI bleed |

## Diagnosis and management

- **Endoscopy** confirms the tear and excludes other bleeding sources.
- Most cases are **self-limiting** and need only supportive care.
- Surgery is rarely required.

<Callout kind="warning" title="Do not confuse with Boerhaave syndrome">
Boerhaave syndrome is a **full-thickness oesophageal rupture** after vomiting. It presents with severe chest pain and sepsis risk, and is a surgical emergency. A Mallory-Weiss tear is mucosal only.
</Callout>

<Callout kind="pearl">
Vomiting first, blood second: think Mallory-Weiss. Bleeding with no preceding retching shifts the differential towards ulcer or varices.
</Callout>
