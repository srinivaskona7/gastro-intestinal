## What it is

**Meckel's diverticulum** is a congenital malformation of the small intestine: a single pouch sticking out of the bowel. It is a **true diverticulum**, meaning that its wall contains **all the layers** of the intestine, unlike acquired false diverticula.

## Causes and pathogenesis

A defect in **embryological development** leaves one diverticulum in the small bowel, positioned just deep to the umbilicus. Because it is congenital, it is present from birth even if it never causes problems.

## The rule of twos

This memory aid captures both the structure and the epidemiology.

| The "two" | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 2 percent | Of the population has one |
| 2 years | Typical age at presentation |
| 2 cm | Length of the diverticulum |
| 2 feet | Within about 2 feet of the ileocaecal valve |
| 2 tissues | Contains gastric and pancreatic tissue |

<Callout kind="pearl">
The ectopic **gastric** tissue can secrete acid. That explains the **ulceration and bleeding** that give the classic painless melaena in a small child.
</Callout>

## Clinical features

Most people with a Meckel's diverticulum are **asymptomatic**. When it does declare itself, usually around age 2, the course is typically:

1. **Melaena** first (passage of black, tarry stool).
2. Later, **severe abdominal pain** from small bowel obstruction, volvulus or intussusception.
3. Some cases **look like appendicitis**.

## Complications

- Bleeding
- Peptic ulceration
- Infection
- Torsion
- Ischaemia and necrosis
- Herniation
- Obstruction

## Diagnosis and treatment

- Diagnosis is **clinical**, supported by **ultrasound or CT**.
- Treatment is **surgery**.

<Callout kind="warning" title="Appendicitis mimic">
A child with appendicitis-like pain, or with melaena and no obvious cause, should have Meckel's diverticulum on the differential.
</Callout>
