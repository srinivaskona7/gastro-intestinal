## What it is

**Haemorrhoids** are enlarged vascular cushions of the anal canal, either **internal** or **external**. The underlying problem is **incompetent valves in the rectal veins**, so blood pools and the vessels swell.

<Anatomy figure="anorectal" highlight={['haemorrhoid-internal', 'haemorrhoid-external']} caption="Anorectal: haemorrhoid internal, haemorrhoid external" />

## Causes and pathogenesis

Anything that causes **chronic raised intra-abdominal pressure** can start the process:

- Chronic constipation
- Pregnancy
- Asthma (chronic coughing)
- Weight lifting

## Clinical features

Haemorrhoids are the **commonest cause of minor rectal bleeding**, accounting for more than half of cases. Whether they hurt depends on type.

| Type | Presentation |
|---|---|
| Internal | Painless rectal bleeding |
| External | Painful rectal bleeding, with or without itching |

## Management

| Line | Treatment |
|---|---|
| First | Dietary fibre, fluids, analgesia, hygiene and rest |
| Second | Surgery |

Surgery is matched to the pain pattern:

- **Internal haemorrhoids**: **banding**, which is tolerable because the area is painless.
- **External haemorrhoids**: **excision under general anaesthetic**, because the area is painful.

<Callout kind="pearl">
Painless bleeding is internal, painful is external. This also decides the operation: banding for internal, excision for external.
</Callout>

<Callout kind="warning">
Do not assume rectal bleeding is haemorrhoids; cancer and polyps cause minor bleeding too, so consider further investigation when appropriate.
</Callout>
