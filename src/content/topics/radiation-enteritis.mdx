## What it is

**Radiation enteritis** is injury to the intestine caused by radiotherapy. Because pelvic cancers are commonly irradiated, the **ileum and rectum** are the most often affected parts.

<Anatomy figure="lower-gi" highlight={['ileum', 'rectum']} caption="Lower gi: ileum, rectum" />

## Pathogenesis

A dose of more than **40 Gray** damages the bowel. The changes follow a progression: muscle fibre atrophy, then ischaemia and ulcerative change, then healing by fibrosis that produces strictures and eventually obstruction.

## Morphology

- Muscle atrophy
- Ulcerative changes
- Fibrotic strictures

## Clinical features

| Phase | Timing | Features |
|---|---|---|
| Acute | Improves within 6 weeks of radiation | Nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea, abdominal pain |
| Chronic | Symptoms for more than 3 months | Mainly pain from obstruction; malabsorption may occur; bowel frequency and tenesmus |

<Callout kind="pearl">
Acute damage settles by itself; the chronic form comes from fibrosis and can appear months or years after treatment.
</Callout>

## Treatment

- **Symptomatic** care, as the acute illness is self-limiting.
- **Surgery only** for obstruction or perforation.

## Complications

It may lead to chronic irritable bowel syndrome.
