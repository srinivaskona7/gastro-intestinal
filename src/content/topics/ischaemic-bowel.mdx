## What it is

**Ischaemic bowel** is injury to the intestine because its blood supply is cut off or inadequate. Left untreated, ischaemia progresses to **infarction** (death of bowel wall).

<Anatomy figure="lower-gi" highlight={['splenic-flexure']} caption="Lower gi: splenic flexure" />

## Causes

- **Mesenteric thromboembolism**, for example from atrial fibrillation (emboli) or atherosclerosis (thrombosis).
- **Aortic dissection** occluding the coeliac trunk, superior mesenteric artery (SMA) or inferior mesenteric artery (IMA).
- **Segmental strangulation**, as in hernia or volvulus.

## Clinical features

| Stage | Features |
|---|---|
| Early | Rapid-onset severe periumbilical pain, nausea and vomiting, forceful defecation, haematochezia if large bowel is involved |
| Later | Peritonitis (guarding, rigidity, rebound tenderness), abdominal distension, absent bowel sounds |

<Callout kind="warning" title="Pain out of proportion">
Severe pain with a soft abdomen early on is typical; peritonism means the bowel is already dying. Do not wait for it.
</Callout>

## Investigations

| Test | Purpose |
|---|---|
| ECG | Looks for AF as the embolic source |
| Coagulation screen | Hypercoagulability |
| Abdominal X-ray | Thickened bowel wall, pneumatosis intestinalis (gas in the wall) |
| CT | Definitive |
| Mesenteric angiogram | Definitive |
| ABG | Metabolic acidosis |

## Management

The key word is **reperfusion**.

- **Anticoagulation** (warfarin with heparin cover) and **thrombolysis** (tPA).
- **Decompression** with a nasogastric tube.
- **Antibiotics**: ampicillin, gentamicin and metronidazole.
- **Surgery** for late signs or perforation: bowel resection and colostomy.
- Optional extras: beta-blocker (carvedilol), and digoxin if AF is present.

## Complications

- Perforation
- Sepsis
- Lactic acidosis
