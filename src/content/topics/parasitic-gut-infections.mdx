## General principles

| Aspect | Detail |
|---|---|
| Groups | Protozoa and helminths (worms) |
| Transmission | Faecal-oral: swallowing dormant **cysts** in contaminated food or water |
| Diagnosis | Stool samples examined for cysts by direct microscopy, or antigen testing |
| Prevention | Boil water to kill cysts, good hygiene, avoid faecal contact |

## Giardia

**Pathogenesis:** Giardia is **not toxigenic**. Instead it **covers the brush border** of the small intestine, which blocks absorption. Its exact mechanism is mostly unknown, but it may provoke an inflammatory response that disrupts the absorptive surface. Villi are damaged, so crypt cells multiply faster to replace them. This leaves **immature cells on the villus**, which absorb poorly.

**Transmission:** ingestion of cysts from contaminated drinking water or public pools. The cysts **survive chlorination**.

**Diagnosis:** cysts in the stool.

**Complications:** chronic infection and malabsorption, leading to malnutrition and **fatty stools**.

**Treatment:** **metronidazole**.

## Cryptosporidium

**Cryptosporidium** is an oocyst-forming protozoan, often confused with Giardia. It is usually **self-limiting in immunocompetent** people and treated with **nitazoxanide** if needed. In **AIDS patients** it does not clear, so it becomes a **chronic infection**. Diagnosis is by stool examination.

## Entamoeba histolytica (amoebic dysentery)

- **Transmission:** faecal-oral ingestion of cysts.
- **Pathogenesis:** invades the intestinal wall, causing **ulceration** and **dysentery** (bloody diarrhoea).
- **Diagnosis:** cysts in stool.
- **Treatment:** **metronidazole**.

## Helminths

The clinically most important group is the **soil-transmitted helminths**, grouped by route of entry.

| Route | Organisms |
|---|---|
| Swallowing eggs | *Ascaris lumbricoides* (roundworm), *Trichuris trichiura* (whipworm) |
| Active skin penetration | *Strongyloides stercoralis* (threadworm), *Ancylostoma duodenale* (hookworm) |

Treatment is **albendazole**.

<Callout kind="pearl">
Metronidazole covers Giardia and Entamoeba; albendazole covers the worms. Bloody diarrhoea points to Entamoeba, fatty pale stools to Giardia.
</Callout>
