## Overview

The pancreas forms from two buds that fuse during development. Errors in this process, or in where tissue ends up, give four distinct conditions.

| Condition | Core defect | Main consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Pancreas divisum | Duct systems fail to fuse | Chronic pancreatitis |
| Annular pancreas | Head encircles duodenum | Duodenal obstruction |
| Ectopic pancreas | Normal tissue in wrong place | Usually none |
| Pancreatic cysts | Fluid collections | Pain, jaundice, malignancy risk |

## Pancreas divisum

Normally the duct systems of the pancreas join so that most secretions leave through the **ampulla of Vater** alongside the bile. In divisum they **fail to fuse**. Strictly, it is the ventral and dorsal pancreatic ducts that fail to join, not the common bile duct and pancreatic duct.

The result is that the bile duct drains through the ampulla of Vater while the bulk of the pancreatic juice must leave through the **minor sphincter (minor papilla) only**, which is **narrower and shorter**. Secretions drain poorly, pressure builds, and this **predisposes to chronic pancreatitis**.

## Annular pancreas

The **head of the pancreas encircles the duodenum**, like a ring around a pipe. This can squeeze the duodenum and cause **duodenal obstruction**.

## Ectopic pancreas

An ectopic pancreas is a **choristoma**: a benign tumour-like mass made of normal tissue in an abnormal location. It consists of **exocrine (acinar) pancreatic tissue** outside the pancreas and may be found in:

- stomach
- duodenum
- jejunum
- Meckel's diverticulum

It is typically a small mass (under 1 cm), **usually asymptomatic**, and **malignancy is rare**.

## Pancreatic cysts

These are **fluid collections within the head, body or tail** of the pancreas. The cause here is congenital. Microscopically they are **epithelial lined** and the contents are fluid.

| Situation | Presentation |
|---|---|
| Small | Asymptomatic |
| Large | Abdominal pain, back pain, duodenal obstruction, jaundice if in the head |
| Infected | Fever, chills, sepsis |

They may be **benign, precancerous or cancerous**, so the nature of each cyst matters.

<Callout kind="pearl">
Annular pancreas obstructs the duodenum from outside; ectopic pancreas is a lump of normal tissue misplaced; divisum is a drainage problem. Keep the mechanism separate for each.
</Callout>

<Callout kind="warning" title="Large cysts in the head">
A cyst in the pancreatic head can compress the bile duct and produce jaundice, which can be mistaken for a tumour.
</Callout>
