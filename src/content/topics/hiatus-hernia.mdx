## What it is

The oesophagus passes through an opening in the diaphragm called the **hiatus**. In a **hiatus hernia**, part of the stomach moves up through that opening and sits above the diaphragm, in the chest.

## Causes and risk factors

Two things combine:

- A **weakness or tear** in the diaphragmatic hiatus.
- Possibly a **raised intra-abdominal pressure** (for example obesity, pregnancy, straining) that pushes the stomach upwards.

## Pathogenesis: two types

| Feature | Sliding hernia | Rolling hernia |
|---|---|---|
| Frequency | About 95% | About 5% |
| What moves | Gastro-oesophageal junction and stomach slide up together | Part of the fundus pushes up beside the oesophagus |
| Gastro-oesophageal junction | Above the diaphragm | Stays in place |
| Lower oesophageal sphincter | Incompetent | Remains competent |
| Main problem | Acid reflux (GORD) | Pain from twisting or strangulation |
| Prevalence | About 30% of people over 50 | Uncommon |

## Clinical features

Most people have **no symptoms**. When symptoms appear they depend on the type and on what the hernia presses on.

- **GORD**: heartburn and regurgitation, typically relieved by standing upright.
- **Pain** from oesophageal strangulation or twisting (rolling type).
- **Shortness of breath**, because the herniated stomach interferes with the diaphragm.
- **Palpitations**, from irritation of the vagus nerve.

## Investigations

| Test | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Gastroscopy with biopsy | Visualise the hernia and rule out Barrett's oesophagus and oesophageal cancer |
| Oesophageal manometry | Measures pressure to detect sphincter incompetence |
| Barium swallow or CT | Shows the stomach lying above the diaphragm |

## Management

1. **Conservative**: weight loss, dietary change, positional measures (such as raising the head of the bed), smaller meals, and avoiding alcohol and caffeine.
2. **Medical**: antacids (for example Mylanta), a proton-pump inhibitor (omeprazole), and an antiemetic (metoclopramide).
3. **Surgery** if severe: **Nissen fundoplication**, done open or laparoscopically, wrapping the upper stomach around the lower oesophagus to strengthen the barrier.

## Complications

- **Barrett's oesophagus**, which may progress to oesophageal cancer (from chronic reflux).
- **Strangulation of the stomach** in rolling hernias, leading to ischaemia and necrosis.

<Callout kind="warning" title="Rolling hernias can strangulate">
A rolling hernia may have no reflux at all because the sphincter still works. Sudden severe pain with a known hernia suggests twisting or strangulation and needs urgent assessment.
</Callout>

<Callout kind="pearl">
Sliding means reflux (sphincter fails); rolling means strangulation (sphincter fine). The common one is sliding.
</Callout>
