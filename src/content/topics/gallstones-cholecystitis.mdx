## Gallstones (cholelithiasis)

### Types

| Type | Share | Appearance | X-ray |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mixed (cholesterol, calcium, bile pigment and blood) | About 80% | Multiple, faceted, yellow-grey | About 20% radio-opaque; high-calcium stones are 100% radio-opaque |
| Pure pigment | Part of the 20% pure group | Dark brown or black, friable, soft | About 50% radio-opaque |
| Pure cholesterol (rare) | Part of the 20% pure group | Yellow and spiky | Usually not radio-opaque |

Pure stones make up about 20% overall (pigment only or cholesterol only). The risk factors differ for each type.

### Pathogenesis: four steps

<Mermaid
  caption="Four mechanisms of stone formation"
  chart={`flowchart LR
  A["1. Supersaturation: excess cholesterol or low bile salts"] --> B["2. Calcium microprecipitation"]
  B --> C["3. Biliary stasis: mucus traps crystals"]
  C --> D[Aggregation]
  D --> E["4. Stone growth"]`}
/>

1. **Supersaturation:** too much cholesterol, or too few bile salts to keep it dissolved.
2. **Calcium microprecipitation:** tiny crystals form.
3. **Biliary stasis:** slow bile flow lets mucus trap the crystals.
4. **Growth:** the crystals aggregate and the stone enlarges.

### Clinical features and tests

- **80% are asymptomatic.** The other 20% develop biliary colic, acute cholecystitis or chronic cholecystitis.
- **Biliary colic:** severe, colicky upper abdominal pain that radiates to the right shoulder, often after a fatty meal. Pain lasts under 3 hours.
- Fat intolerance, with clay-coloured stools when bile cannot reach the gut.
- **Labs:** raised conjugated bilirubin (extrahepatic obstruction) and raised alkaline phosphatase.

### Management and complications

Management: nil by mouth (bowel rest), IV rehydration, analgesia and IV antibiotics.

Complications: cholangitis, pancreatitis, cholecystitis and cholangiocarcinoma.

## Acute cholecystitis

Inflammation of the gallbladder, with biliary pain lasting **over 3 hours**. It is more typical in females.

| | Calculous (90%) | Acalculous (10%) |
|---|---|---|
| Cause | Gallstone blocks the cystic duct | No stones; seen in severely ill or trauma patients |
| Risk factors | Age, female sex, obesity, rapid weight loss, drugs, pregnancy | Critical illness or sepsis, major surgery, prolonged fasting |
| Mechanism | Obstruction leads to bile stasis and distension | Prolonged parenteral feeding leads to bile stasis and distension |
| Next steps | Secondary infection by gut organisms (E. coli), then inflammation | Same |

**Morphology:** a large, red, swollen gallbladder that may be felt as a right upper quadrant mass.

### Clinical features

- **Early:** right upper quadrant colicky pain radiating to the right scapula, worse with food; nausea, vomiting, fever and tachycardia.
- **Later (hours):** severe constant RUQ pain with peritonitis (rigidity, guarding, rebound tenderness); **Murphy sign** (inspiration suddenly halts under the palpating hand); RUQ mass; jaundice.

### Diagnosis, treatment, complications

- Raised alkaline phosphatase (biliary obstruction).
- **Abdominal ultrasound is definitive.** ERCP is an alternative that is also therapeutic.
- Treatment: nil by mouth, IV fluids, analgesia, IV antibiotics and **cholecystectomy**.
- Complications: acute gangrenous cholecystitis (very high pressure compromises blood supply), empyema from secondary infection, and gallbladder perforation. About 30% need surgery.

<Callout kind="pearl">
Biliary colic versus cholecystitis comes down to duration and inflammation: pain under 3 hours is colic, over 3 hours with fever, tenderness and Murphy sign is cholecystitis.
</Callout>

## Recurrent or chronic cholecystitis

Caused by **biliary gravel**: thick bile with many small stones. A repeated cycle of bile stasis, stone formation and obstruction gives chronic inflammation and mild transient obstructions, so discomfort follows meals.

- **Features:** chronic vague RUQ or upper abdominal discomfort after meals, indigestion, upper abdominal distension.
- **Management:** cholecystectomy.

<Callout kind="warning" title="Do not wait on gangrene">
Constant severe pain with peritonism suggests gangrene or perforation. This needs urgent surgical review.
</Callout>
