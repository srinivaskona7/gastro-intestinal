## What it is

**Cholangiocarcinoma** (bile duct carcinoma) is a malignant tumour of the bile ducts. Microscopically it is an **adenocarcinoma**. It can start at any point along the biliary tree, inside or outside the liver.

<Anatomy figure="biliary-pancreas" highlight={['hepatic-ducts', 'common-bile-duct']} caption="Biliary pancreas: hepatic ducts, common bile duct" />

## Causes / risk factors

The common thread is long-standing irritation or abnormality of the ducts.

| Risk factor | Notes |
|---|---|
| Bile duct cysts | Congenital abnormality of the ducts |
| Primary sclerosing cholangitis | Chronic inflammation of the bile ducts |
| Ulcerative colitis | Linked through its association with sclerosing cholangitis |
| History of parasitic worm infection | Chronic biliary infestation |

<Callout kind="pearl">
Cholangiocarcinoma is **not** associated with hepatitis or cirrhosis. That sets it apart from hepatocellular carcinoma.
</Callout>

## Pathogenesis

A neoplasm develops in the lining of the ducts. As it grows it narrows the lumen, which causes **biliary obstruction**, and obstruction produces most of the symptoms.

## Clinical features

- Fever and chills
- Anorexia and weight loss
- **Post-hepatic (obstructive) jaundice**: yellow skin and eyes, **dark urine** (conjugated bilirubin spills into urine) and **pale stools** (no bile pigment reaches the gut)

## Investigations

- **ALP and GGT raised**, the cholestatic pattern. ALT and AST may be mildly raised, and conjugated bilirubin may be mildly raised.
- **CT** for diagnosis and staging.

## Management and prognosis

- **Surgery** if the tumour is extrahepatic.
- **Liver transplant** if the tumour is large.
- Prognosis is **poor**: about **30 percent** survive five years.

<Callout kind="warning" title="Obstructive jaundice needs a cause">
Jaundice with dark urine, pale stools and a raised ALP means the flow of bile is blocked. In a patient with sclerosing cholangitis or ulcerative colitis, keep cholangiocarcinoma on the list.
</Callout>
