## Water absorption

Less than 170 mL of water leaves the gut each day, so nearly everything that enters is absorbed into the blood. Water moves by **osmosis** towards the more concentrated (hypertonic) side, usually following sodium ions though not always. It crosses through **aquaporins**, water-specific channel proteins.

| Site | Mechanism |
|---|---|
| Jejunum | Na+/K+ ATPase keeps intracellular sodium low. SGLT1 imports sodium and glucose together, water follows, and basal GLUT2 releases glucose to the interstitium |
| Ileum | Na+/K+ ATPase again; sodium enters through a Na+/H+ antiporter (H+ goes to the lumen); bicarbonate enters the lumen through a HCO3-/Cl- antiporter, balancing charge; chloride moves from cell to interstitium to balance sodium |
| Proximal colon | Na+/K+ ATPase; sodium enters through an apical ion channel; excess potassium leaks back to the interstitium through leak channels |

<Callout kind="pearl" title="Why oral rehydration works">
In severe dehydration a solution containing both sodium and glucose gives the best water uptake, because SGLT1 couples the two.
</Callout>

## Carbohydrate

Intake is about 200-600 g a day and supplies roughly half of energy needs, at 16 kJ per gram. Complex carbohydrates give the slowest energy release.

### Digestion

| Site | Enzyme | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Mouth | Salivary alpha-amylase (parotid, submandibular) | Cuts starch to short chains of a few units; needs the right pH, so works in the mouth but not the stomach |
| Small intestine lumen | Pancreatic alpha-amylase | Cuts starch into even smaller pieces. Its release is stimulated by CCK; secretin drives bicarbonate release that neutralises chyme to the optimum pH |
| Brush border | Membrane-bound enzymes | Oligosaccharides and disaccharides to monosaccharides |

Brush-border enzymes are often bifunctional, with two active sites in one protein.

| Enzyme | Substrate | Products |
|---|---|---|
| Sucrase | Sucrose | Glucose and fructose |
| Lactase | Lactose | Glucose and galactose |
| Maltase | Maltose | Glucose and glucose |
| Isomaltase | Maltotriose and branch-point fragments | Glucose (not galactose, a common exam trap) |

### Absorption

None occurs in mouth, stomach or large intestine. In the small intestine:

- **Glucose and galactose** enter by the sodium-coupled apical transporter **SGLT1** (secondary active transport; not GLUT1) and exit basally by GLUT2.
- **Fructose** enters by apical GLUT5 and exits by GLUT2.

### Blood glucose fate

| Pathway | Examples |
|---|---|
| Insulin-dependent | Fat synthesis and storage; uptake into muscle (for glycogen storage; storing is glycogenesis, not glycogenolysis) |
| Insulin-independent | Uptake into the liver; uptake into the brain as fuel |

If the insulin-dependent routes are blocked, as in diabetes, blood glucose rises and glucose spills into the urine.

### Glycaemic index and fibre

The **glycaemic index** measures how quickly a carbohydrate releases glucose: high GI means fast, low GI means slow.

| Type | Features |
|---|---|
| Soluble fibre (non-starch polysaccharide, not cellulose) | In fruit, vegetables, oats, seeds; thickens gut contents; fermented by colonic bacteria |
| Insoluble fibre (cellulose) | Plant cell walls; bulks colonic contents and keeps bowels regular; mostly not fermented |
| Resistant starch | Escapes small-bowel digestion, reaches the colon, acts like both fibre types; not strictly fibre |

## Protein

The recommended intake is about 125 g a day (sources: meat, eggs, dairy, seeds, nuts, legumes). Proteins supply amino acids; **essential** ones must be eaten, **non-essential** ones can be made by the body.

### Digestion

1. **Stomach:** chief cells secrete pepsinogen and parietal cells secrete HCl. Acid converts it to **pepsin**, an endopeptidase that breaks bonds inside proteins to give smaller polypeptides.
2. **Small intestine:** proteases cut polypeptides to single amino acids.

Because pepsin is released as a **zymogen**, an inactive precursor needing a chemical change to activate, it does not digest the cells that made it.

<Callout kind="pearl" title="Newborns">
Newborns cannot digest protein well, which spares IgA antibodies in colostrum. They protect the gut and are also taken into the blood for wider immunity.
</Callout>

### Absorption

Single amino acids and some di- and tripeptides are taken up mainly in the small intestine by enterocytes, by cotransport with sodium. Intracellular peptidases finish breaking down di- and tripeptides, and basolateral transporters release amino acids and peptides into villus capillaries.

## Lipids

Intake is 60-100 g a day: 90% triglycerides, 10% cholesterol, cholesterol esters, phospholipids and the fat-soluble vitamins A, D, E and K.

### Emulsification

Fats do not dissolve in water. Gastric contractions spread fat evenly through chyme. In the duodenum, **bile salts**, which have a polar end and a non-polar end (amphiphilic), break large droplets into tiny ones. More surface area means more access for lipases.

### Digestion

- **Gastric lipase** comes from chief cells; **pancreatic lipase** is secreted in active form. Both are water-soluble and hydrolyse the ester bonds between glycerol and fatty acids.
- Usually the result is one monoacylglyceride plus two free fatty acids; rarely it is glycerol plus three fatty acids.

### Absorption

<Mermaid
  caption="From lumen to lymph"
  chart={`flowchart TD
  A["Micelle at brush border"] --> B["Fatty acids and monoglycerides diffuse into enterocyte"]
  B --> C["Smooth ER rebuilds triglycerides"]
  C --> D["Golgi packs with cholesterol and lipoproteins"]
  D --> E["Chylomicron"]
  E --> F["Exocytosed to interstitium"]
  F --> G["Lacteal then lymphatics then blood"]`}
/>

1. Monoglycerides and fatty acids stay associated with bile acids and form **micelles** in the chyme.
2. At the brush border, micelles release their cargo, which diffuses freely into the enterocyte.
3. In the endoplasmic reticulum they are rebuilt into triglycerides, then packaged in the Golgi with cholesterol and lipoproteins into **chylomicrons** (the lowest-density lipoprotein).
4. Vesicles carry chylomicrons to the basolateral membrane, where they are exocytosed, enter the **lacteal** in the villus, pass through lymph and reach the blood. There they are rapidly used.

**Cholesterol** is absorbed in the small intestine through a specific transporter and carried in chylomicrons. Homeostasis is the balance of synthesis, absorption and excretion in bile.
