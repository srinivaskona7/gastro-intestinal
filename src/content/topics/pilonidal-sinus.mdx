## What it is

A **pilonidal sinus** is a small tract, cyst or abscess found in or near the **natal cleft**, the groove between the buttocks. The cavity typically contains **hair and skin debris**. The name comes from Latin and means **"nest of hair"**, which describes what is found inside.

A cyst can stay quiet, but when it becomes infected it forms a painful **pilonidal abscess**.

## Causes and risk factors

| Factor | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Ingrown hair | Loose hairs bore into the skin of the cleft and set up a foreign-body reaction |
| Excessive sitting | Pressure and friction drive hair into the skin |
| Excessive sweating | Moist, warm skin favours blockage and infection |

## Clinical features

- The lesion is **very painful**, especially on sitting.
- It is **more common in men than women**.
- It is seen **mostly in adolescents** and young adults.

## Management

**Excision** of the sinus or cyst is the treatment. An acutely infected abscess needs to be drained as part of this surgical approach.

Young male, painful lump over the lower sacrum or natal cleft, hair visible: think pilonidal disease, not a perianal abscess.
