## What it is

**Pancreatic adenocarcinoma** is a cancer of the **exocrine, ductal** part of the pancreas. It arises through carcinogenesis in the exocrine glands and accounts for most pancreatic cancers.

## Risk factors

- Smoking
- Alcohol
- Chronic pancreatitis
- Diabetes
- Familial syndromes

## Clinical features

The disease is typically in **older patients (60 to 80 years)** and is **asymptomatic until advanced**. Around **60% occur in the head of the pancreas**, so they present early with signs of **common bile duct obstruction**, such as jaundice. Body and tail tumours stay silent longer.

| Sign or symptom | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Mid-epigastric pain radiating to the back | Local invasion |
| Obstructive jaundice | Bile duct blocked |
| Steatorrhoea | Lack of bile and pancreatic enzymes in the gut |
| Palpable gallbladder (Courvoisier sign) | Distended by distal obstruction |
| Migratory thrombophlebitis (Trousseau syndrome) | Cancer-related clotting tendency |
| Diabetes | Loss of endocrine function |
| Anorexia and weight loss, extreme fatigue, depression | Systemic effects of advanced cancer |

<Callout kind="pearl">
A painless, jaundiced patient with a palpable gallbladder is pancreatic cancer until proven otherwise. In gallstone obstruction the gallbladder is usually scarred and not palpable.
</Callout>

## Investigations

- **CT or ultrasound** to find and stage the tumour.
- **CEA (carcino-embryonic antigen)** is used to monitor treatment. In current practice CA 19-9 is the usual marker for this purpose.

## Treatment

- **Surgery**: the **Whipple procedure** for tumours of the head, or **distal pancreatectomy** for tail tumours.
- **Palliative chemotherapy** for disease that cannot be resected.

## Complications and prognosis

Complications are **metastases** and **obstructive jaundice**. Because the disease is advanced at diagnosis, outlook is **very poor**: about **25% survive one year** and **5% survive five years**.

<Callout kind="warning" title="Late presentation">
Weight loss, back pain and new-onset diabetes in an older person deserve imaging, because early disease is silent and curable surgery is only possible in a minority.
</Callout>
