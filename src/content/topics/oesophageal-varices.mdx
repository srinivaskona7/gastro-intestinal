## What it is

**Oesophageal varices** are engorged, tortuous veins in the **lower oesophagus**, resembling varicose veins. They are a result of **portal hypertension**, raised pressure in the portal venous system.

<Anatomy figure="portal-system" highlight={['oesophageal-collaterals']} caption="Portal system: oesophageal collaterals" />

## Causes

Portal hypertension is classified by where the block lies relative to the liver:

| Level | Cause |
|---|---|
| Pre-hepatic | Portal vein thrombosis |
| Intrahepatic | Cirrhosis (the commonest) |
| Post-hepatic | Hepatic vein obstruction |

## Pathogenesis

When blood cannot pass through the liver, pressure builds in the portal vein. Blood finds an alternative route by opening **porta-systemic shunts**, connections between the portal and systemic veins. At the lower oesophagus this blood flows into the oesophageal veins, which are thin-walled and not built for the load, so they swell into varices.

## Clinical features

| Group | Features |
|---|---|
| Bleeding | Haematemesis, melaena |
| Anaemia | Dyspnoea, dizziness, light-headedness, syncope |
| Cirrhotic | Jaundice, confusion, hepatic flap, small nodular liver |
| Alcohol-related | Intoxication, Dupuytren's contracture |

The cirrhotic and alcohol features are clues to the underlying cause, not to the varices themselves.

## Investigations

| Test | Finding |
|---|---|
| LFTs | Raised transaminases, low albumin, raised ALP and GGT |
| FBC | Anaemia |
| B12 and folate | Deficiency if alcoholic |
| Urea, electrolytes and creatinine (UEC) | Electrolyte disturbance |
| **Endoscopy** | **Definitive diagnosis** |

## Management

- **Thiamine replacement** plus B12 and folate (for alcohol-related deficiency).
- **PPI** (omeprazole).
- Procedure if severe or actively bleeding: **endoscopic band ligation**.

<Callout kind="warning" title="Emergency management">
Active variceal bleeding is an emergency: resuscitation, vasoactive drugs (terlipressin or octreotide) and antibiotics are standard alongside band ligation.
</Callout>

## Complications

Rupture leads to **massive GI bleeding**, then **shock and death**.

<Callout kind="pearl">
Haematemesis in a patient with stigmata of chronic liver disease (jaundice, flap, small nodular liver, Dupuytren's) should make you think varices first, and endoscopy confirms.
</Callout>
