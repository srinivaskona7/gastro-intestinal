## What they are

The endocrine pancreas is made of the islets of Langerhans, small clusters of hormone-producing cells scattered through the gland. Tumours arising from them are called **islet cell tumours** (or pancreatic adenomas when they are benign). The key points:

- The **majority are benign**.
- They matter because of the **hormone** they pour out, not because of mass effect.
- Each tumour type produces one dominant hormone and therefore its own syndrome.

The overall treatment options are:

1. **Monitoring** for small, quiet lesions.
2. **Surgery** to remove the tumour.
3. **Hormone therapy** when surgery is not viable.
4. **Radiation therapy, nuclear medicine or radiofrequency ablation** as further options for tumours that cannot be resected.

## Gastrinoma (Zollinger-Ellison syndrome)

A gastrinoma is a tumour that secretes **gastrin**. Gastrin drives the stomach to make acid, so the tumour produces severe, unrelenting hyperacidity.

<Mermaid
  caption="How a gastrinoma produces ulcers and diarrhoea"
  chart={`flowchart LR
  A[Gastrinoma] --> B[Excess gastrin]
  B --> C[Gastric acid hypersecretion]
  C --> D[Peptic ulcers]
  C --> E["Acid inactivates pancreatic lipase"]
  C --> F[Mucosal damage]
  E --> G[Chronic diarrhoea]
  F --> G`}
/>

| Aspect | Detail |
|---|---|
| Clinical features | Abdominal pain, dyspepsia, chronic diarrhoea |
| Why diarrhoea | Excess acid inactivates pancreatic enzymes such as lipase, and also damages the intestinal mucosa |
| Diagnosis | Fasting serum gastrin; endoscopic ultrasound and CT to image and stage the tumour |
| Treatment | Proton pump inhibitor (omeprazole); surgical removal of the gastrinoma, with about 80% five-year survival |
| Complication | Malignant potential |

<Callout kind="pearl">
Think gastrinoma when ulcers are multiple, recurrent, or refractory to treatment, especially with diarrhoea. The tumour is the cause, so the acid must be controlled with a PPI while the tumour is dealt with.
</Callout>

## Insulinoma

An insulinoma is a pancreatic tumour that makes **too much insulin**. Normal beta cells switch insulin off when glucose falls; tumour cells do not, so insulin keeps being released even when the blood sugar is already too low.

- **Morphology:** usually a single, small tumour in adults.
- **Mild hypoglycaemia:** anxiety, tachycardia, sweating, hunger and headache (the adrenergic warning symptoms).
- **Severe hypoglycaemia:** seizures, coma and even death.

| Aspect | Detail |
|---|---|
| Pathogenesis | Unregulated insulin production leading to hypoglycaemia |
| Diagnosis | Blood sugar level with a simultaneous insulin level; CT or MRI of the abdomen; endoscopic ultrasound |
| Treatment | Surgery, which is curative because these tumours are rarely malignant |

<Callout kind="warning" title="Do not miss the low sugar">
A blood glucose should be checked at the time of symptoms. The diagnosis rests on finding inappropriately high insulin while the glucose is low.
</Callout>

## Multiple endocrine neoplasia (MEN)

MEN syndromes are inherited conditions in which tumours arise in several endocrine glands. Two types are described, and islet cell tumours feature in type 1.

<Mermaid
  caption="Glands involved in MEN1 and MEN2"
  chart={`flowchart TD
  M[Multiple endocrine neoplasia] --> M1["MEN1 - Wermer syndrome"]
  M[Multiple endocrine neoplasia] --> M2["MEN2 - Sipple syndrome"]
  M1 --> P1[Pituitary adenoma]
  M1 --> P2["Pancreatic gastrinoma or insulinoma"]
  M1 --> P3[Parathyroid adenoma]
  M2 --> T1[Medullary thyroid cancer]
  M2 --> T2[Phaeochromocytoma]
  M2 --> T3[Parathyroid hyperplasia or adenoma]`}
/>

### MEN1 (Wermer syndrome)

Caused by a mutation in the **MEN1 tumour suppressor gene**. About 60% of patients have at least two of the following:

| Tumour | Consequence |
|---|---|
| Pituitary adenoma | Bilateral hemianopia (visual field loss from compression of the optic chiasm) |
| Pancreatic gastrinoma | Peptic ulcer disease |
| Pancreatic insulinoma | Hypoglycaemia |
| Parathyroid adenomas | Primary hyperparathyroidism |

### MEN2 (Sipple syndrome)

Caused by a genetic mutation in the MEN2 gene (a gain-of-function mutation of the RET proto-oncogene, not a tumour suppressor). Frequency of findings:

| Tumour | Frequency |
|---|---|
| Medullary thyroid cancer | 100% |
| Phaeochromocytoma (adrenaline-secreting) | 50% |
| Parathyroid hyperplasia or adenoma (PTH-secreting) | 30% |

<Callout kind="pearl">
Medullary thyroid cancer is not thyroid hormone secreting: it arises from C cells and secretes calcitonin, so it does not cause thyrotoxicosis. Pancreatic tumours belong to MEN1, not MEN2.
</Callout>
