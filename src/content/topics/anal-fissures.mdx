## What it is

An **anal fissure** is a **crack or tear in the skin of the anal canal**. It extends outwards from the anal opening and may be **superficial or deep**. About **80% are posterior**.

## Causes

The lining tears when it is **over-stretched**.

| Group | Triggers |
|---|---|
| Adults | Constipation, prolonged diarrhoea, Crohn disease, post-partum, anal sex |
| Older adults | Relative ischaemia of the anal lining |

## Clinical features

In order of frequency:

1. **Pain** (most prominent)
2. **Bleeding**
3. **Itch**
4. Constipation
5. Discharge

## Differential diagnosis

A fissure that looks atypical should prompt thought of other causes of anal ulceration:

- **Crohn ulcer**
- **Syphilis**
- **Herpes**
- **Cancer**

<Callout kind="warning">
A fissure that is off the midline, multiple, or fails to heal should make you think of Crohn disease, infection or malignancy.
</Callout>

## Management

- **Topical moisturisers** for new fissures.
- **Chronic** fissures: surgery, specifically **lateral internal sphincterotomy**.

<Callout kind="pearl">
Fissure causes painful bleeding (pain first), unlike painless internal haemorrhoid bleeding. It accounts for about 18% of minor rectal bleeds.
</Callout>
