## What it is

**Acute cholangitis** is infection of the bile ducts. Bile is normally sterile and flows downhill into the duodenum. When flow stops, bacteria can climb from the gut into the biliary tree and multiply in the trapped bile.

## Causes and pathogenesis

- **Choledocholithiasis** (a stone in the common bile duct) is the commonest cause of obstruction.
- Infection is by gut bacteria, typically **E. coli** or **Klebsiella**.
- **Biliary stasis** is the trigger. Besides stones, causes include obstruction, anorexia (no food stimulating bile flow) and total parenteral nutrition (TPN, which leaves the gut unused).

## Clinical features

| Scoring | Components |
|---|---|
| Charcot's triad | 1. Fever, 2. Jaundice, 3. Abdominal pain |
| Reynolds' pentad | The triad plus 4. Hypotension and 5. Confusion |

The pentad signals severe disease with systemic shock.

<Callout kind="warning" title="The pentad is an emergency">
Hypotension and confusion mean the infection has become septic. Treat urgently and arrange early biliary drainage.
</Callout>

## Investigations

- **FBC:** raised white cell count.
- **LFTs:** an obstructive pattern.
- **Amylase:** raised if pancreatitis is also present (gallstone pancreatitis).
- **Blood cultures** to identify the organism.
- **Ultrasound** to look for stones and duct dilatation.

## Management

1. **IV fluids** to restore circulation.
2. **Antibiotics**: a typical combination is ampicillin, gentamicin and metronidazole.
3. **ERCP or MRCP**: MRCP confirms the diagnosis; ERCP is both diagnostic and therapeutic, allowing stenting or stone extraction to relieve the obstruction.
4. **Cholecystectomy** may follow, to remove the origin of future stones.

<Callout kind="pearl">
Antibiotics alone do not clear an infected obstructed system. Drainage of the duct is the definitive step.
</Callout>

## Complications

**Sepsis** is the main danger, which is why blood cultures, fluids and early drainage matter.
