## Overview

Infectious gastroenteritis produces diarrhoea, often with vomiting. The mechanism differs by organism: some release **toxins**, some **invade** the bowel wall, and viruses **destroy enterocytes**.

## Toxigenic diarrhoea (food poisoning)

| Aspect | Detail |
|---|---|
| Organisms | *Staphylococcus aureus* (poor food handling) and *Bacillus cereus* (mostly cereal, i.e. rice and grain foods) |
| Mechanism | Toxins cause the illness. Some toxins are **heat stable**, so cooking does not make food safe |
| Onset | Within about 4 hours of eating |
| Symptoms | Prominent vomiting, stomach cramps, diarrhoea |
| Diagnosis | History and clinical course. Retrospective epidemiology looks for the common denominator: who ate what? Stool ova, cysts and parasites (OCP) examination if concerned |
| Treatment | Supportive: fluid and electrolyte replacement. Anti-diarrhoeals are controversial: they ease symptoms but slow expulsion of the toxin |

## Escherichia coli types

| Type | Behaviour | Result |
|---|---|---|
| ETEC (enterotoxigenic) | Produces toxins | Traveller's diarrhoea |
| EIEC (enteroinvasive) | Actively invades and destroys intestinal lining | Traveller's dysentery |
| EPEC (enteropathogenic) | Sporadic disease in babies and children | Infant diarrhoea |
| EHEC (enterohaemorrhagic) | Produces verotoxin; destroys platelets and red cells | Haemolytic-uraemic syndrome: kidney failure, bleeding and dysentery |

<Callout kind="warning" title="EHEC is the serious one">
Verotoxin triggers haemolytic-uraemic syndrome: haemolysis, low platelets and acute kidney failure after bloody diarrhoea.
</Callout>

## Salmonella (typhoid)

*Salmonella typhi* causes dysentery and can spread to cause **septicaemia**. The classic picture includes fever, **rose spots** on the skin, delirium and **perforation of the bowel**. Management is **ceftriaxone, with or without ciprofloxacin**.

## Cholera

*Vibrio cholerae* causes **profuse rice-water stools**. Management is **fluid replacement**, and the illness is **self-limiting**.

## Dysenteric organisms

Dysentery means bloody diarrhoea from mucosal invasion. The organisms to remember are **Salmonella, Shigella and Entamoeba histolytica**.

## Viral gastroenteritis

- **Norovirus** causes about 80% of adult cases.
- **Rotavirus** is the main cause in children under 3, spread in day-care centres.
- Spread is **faecal-oral**.

**Pathogenesis:** the virus destroys enterocytes. Rotavirus also produces a toxic protein, **NSP4**, which stimulates chloride secretion and so blocks water absorption in the gut.

| Feature | Detail |
|---|---|
| Incubation | About 2 days |
| Symptom duration | About 6 days |
| Infective period | Remains infectious for about 2 days after symptoms end |
| Symptoms | Projectile vomiting, diarrhoea, and a flu-like illness: fever, irritability, poor feeding, myalgia |
| Diagnosis | Clinical. Definitive by stool enzyme immunoassay or RT-PCR |

Because of the infective tail, a child with vomiting and diarrhoea should stay home for **more than a week** to limit spread.

### Management

Supportive, with **fluid replacement** as the priority. The **"421 rule"** gives maintenance fluid per hour:

| Body weight band | Rate |
|---|---|
| First 10 kg | 4 ml/kg/hr |
| Second 10 kg | 2 ml/kg/hr |
| Each kg thereafter | 1 ml/kg/hr |

**Quarantine** matters, especially around immunocompromised or chemotherapy patients.

## Listeriosis

*Listeria monocytogenes* is a **Gram-positive** bacterium found in **soft cheeses and cold deli meats**. It is a particular risk to **pregnant women and immunocompromised people**.
