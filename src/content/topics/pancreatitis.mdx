## Acute pancreatitis

### What it is

Acute pancreatitis is inflammation caused by the pancreas **digesting itself** (autodigestion). The inflammation is reversible and may include **necrosis**. In severe cases it triggers a **systemic inflammatory response syndrome**.

### Causes

| Cause | Share | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Gallstones | about 50% | Stone obstructs the ampulla or common bile duct |
| Alcohol | about 40% | |
| Others | about 10% | Infections, metabolic (hypercalcaemia from hyperparathyroidism, DKA, uraemia, pregnancy), trauma, ischaemia, duodenal ulcer, scorpion venom, drugs, unidentified |

### Clinical features

This is an **acute medical emergency**.

- Epigastric or abdominal pain, brought on by a large meal or alcohol
- Vomiting
- Peritonitis with guarding and rigidity
- With haemorrhage: hypotension and shock, and **Grey Turner's sign** (flank bruising) and **Cullen's sign** (periumbilical bruising)

### Complications

| Local | Systemic |
|---|---|
| Pancreatic abscess or infection | Jaundice |
| Pseudocysts | DIC (disseminated intravascular coagulation) |
| Duodenal obstruction | ARDS |
| | Acute renal failure |

### Diagnosis

1. **Exclude other causes of acute abdomen**: appendicitis, diverticulitis, peptic ulcer, cholecystitis, ischaemic bowel and bowel obstruction.
2. **Serum amylase** is raised within 24 hours.
3. **Serum lipase** is raised too. Lipase rises early and stays high longer, which makes it useful for late presenters.
4. **FBC**: neutrophil leucocytosis.
5. **Alkaline phosphatase** raised if biliary stasis; **bilirubin** raised.
6. **ERCP or MRCP** if indicated.

<Callout kind="warning" title="Never biopsy">
Biopsy of the inflamed pancreas is hazardous and is not part of the diagnosis.
</Callout>

### Prognosis and treatment

About **80%** are self-limiting with supportive treatment. About **20%** are life threatening with one or more organ failures and need ICU.

- **Supportive**: nil by mouth, fluids, electrolytes, analgesia.
- **Aggressive** for severe disease or organ failure: ICU admission, prophylactic antibiotics if there is necrosis, and rarely surgery.

## Chronic pancreatitis

### What it is

Repeated bouts of mild to moderate pancreatitis that leave **exocrine atrophy and fibrosis**.

### Causes and pathogenesis

**Alcohol abuse** is the main cause. Others are biliary disease, hypercalcaemia, pancreas divisum, familial pancreatitis and cystic fibrosis. Alcohol or another cause obstructs the ducts, which leads to autodigestion and pancreatitis again and again.

### Clinical features

- Intermittent pain
- Weight loss
- Steatorrhoea
- Jaundice
- Secondary diabetes

Diagnosis follows the same approach as acute disease: exclude other acute abdomens, then check amylase, lipase, FBC, alkaline phosphatase and bilirubin.

### Complications

Progressive destruction of the gland lowers function in two ways.

<Mermaid
  caption="Loss of pancreatic function"
  chart={`flowchart LR
  A["Progressive pancreatic destruction"] --> B["Exocrine failure"]
  A --> C["Endocrine failure"]
  B --> D["Enzyme lack, nutritional malabsorption"]
  C --> E["Less insulin and glucagon, diabetes mellitus"]`}
/>

Other complications: **pseudocysts, duct obstruction and pancreatic cancer**.

### Treatment

- **Supportive**: nil by mouth when needed, fluids, electrolytes, analgesia.
- Manage **bile duct disease** if present.
- If the pancreas has failed: **pancreatic enzyme replacement (Creon Forte)** plus **insulin**.

<Callout kind="pearl">
Acute pancreatitis is reversible; chronic pancreatitis is not. Chronic disease costs the patient both digestion (steatorrhoea) and glucose control (diabetes).
</Callout>
