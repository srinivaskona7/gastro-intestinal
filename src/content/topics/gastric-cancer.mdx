## What it is

Gastric cancer is a malignant tumour of the stomach. The great majority are **adenocarcinomas** (glandular neoplasms).

<Anatomy figure="stomach" highlight={['antrum', 'lesser-curvature']} caption="Stomach: antrum, lesser curvature" />

## Causes and pathogenesis

| Type | Share |
|---|---|
| Adenocarcinoma (from H. pylori related metaplasia) | About 90% |
| MALT lymphoma | About 5% |
| Carcinoid, GI stromal tumour (GIST), others | Rare |

## Morphology

"Fungating" describes a lesion marked by ulceration and necrosis.

| Pattern | Appearance |
|---|---|
| Fungating (polypoid) | Polypoid, ulcerating lesion with heaped-up, rolled edges |
| Diffuse | Fibrotic scarring thickens the wall, giving a "leather bottle" stomach |

## Clinical features

- Often **asymptomatic until advanced**, hence the poor prognosis.
- **Early or presenting symptoms**: epigastric pain, nausea, haematemesis, weight loss, lymphadenopathy, anaemia.
- **Late symptoms**: malignant ascites, jaundice, and features of brain, bone or lung metastases.

## Investigations

- **Gastroscopy with biopsy** to confirm and grade.
- **CT** to look for metastases and stage.
- Tumour markers are for monitoring only, not diagnosis.

## Management

- **Surgery**: total gastrectomy with oesophago-jejunostomy, plus sentinel node resection, sometimes with splenectomy.
- Palliative chemotherapy and/or palliative radiotherapy in some patients.

## Prognosis

Poor: under 30% five-year survival even with surgery.

<Callout kind="warning" title="Late presentation">
Persistent dyspepsia with weight loss or anaemia, especially in older patients, warrants endoscopy rather than empirical treatment.
</Callout>

<Callout kind="pearl">
H. pylori leads to chronic gastritis, then metaplasia, then adenocarcinoma. Tumour markers only monitor; they never diagnose.
</Callout>
