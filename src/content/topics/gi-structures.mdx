## Oesophagus

- **Length and course:** about 25 cm; it pierces the diaphragm at the oesophageal hiatus and joins the stomach at the cardiac orifice.
- **Sphincter:** the gastro-oesophageal sphincter. When it malfunctions the result is heartburn, GORD or GERD.
- **Mucosa:** a common mistake is to describe the lower two thirds as columnar. The whole oesophagus is lined by stratified squamous epithelium, changing abruptly at the **oesophago-gastric junction (Z-line)** to gastric columnar epithelium.
- **Submucosa:** dense connective tissue.
- **Muscularis externa:** two opposing layers for peristalsis (striated in the upper third, smooth in the lower two thirds, as given in the overview topic).
- **Adventitia:** instead of a serosa, a purely connective tissue outer covering.

## Stomach

Functions: temporary food storage, mechanical mixing and churning, and chemical digestion with hydrochloric acid.

Other macro features: longitudinal and circular muscle layers, lesser and greater curvatures, and **rugae** (ridges or folds).

### Histology

The surface and **gastric pits** are lined by epithelium protected by mucus and alkaline secretion, with goblet-type mucus cells (simple columnar). The glands contain:

| Cell | Product or role |
|---|---|
| Parietal | Acid and intrinsic factor (needed for B12 absorption) |
| Chief | Pepsinogen, precursor of pepsin, which digests proteins including collagen |
| G cells | Stimulate parietal cells |
| D cells | Inhibit G cells |

### Motility, emptying and vomiting

- Peristalsis: 3 waves per minute moving down the body, very intense at the pylorus, which grinds food into chyme. Each wave delivers about 3 ml to the duodenum.
- **Gastric emptying** is controlled by the duodenum. Fatty food, alcohol, caffeine and smoking delay it. Normally the duodenal contents matter more than gastric contents:
  1. Acidic chyme enters the duodenum.
  2. Chemical and stretch receptors in its wall trigger enterogastric reflexes.
  3. These reflexes reduce acid and pepsin secretion.
  4. They also weaken pyloric contractions, preventing further duodenal filling.
- **Vomiting reflex:** triggered by extreme stomach stretch or irritants (bacterial toxins, alcohol, spicy food, drugs). Blood-borne molecules or sensory signals reach the **emetic centre** in the medulla. The diaphragm contracts, the cardiac sphincter relaxes and the soft palate rises to seal the nasal passage.

### The three phases of acid secretion

<Mermaid
  caption="Phases of gastric secretion"
  chart={`flowchart LR
  A["Cephalic: taste, smell, thought"] --> B["Gastric: food distends stomach"]
  B --> C["Intestinal: acidic chyme in duodenum"]
  C --> D["Secretion inhibited"]`}
/>

| Phase | Trigger | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| Cephalic (before meal) | Taste, smell or thought of food | Signals to medulla, then vagus nerves; parasympathetic neurons drive HCl and pepsin release in upper and middle stomach; G cells release gastrin, which raises parietal HCl and chief cell pepsin output |
| Gastric (about 3-4 h) | Food distends the stomach | Stretch receptors give positive feedback to the vagal reflex; gastrin from G cells is triggered by parasympathetic nerves, partly digested protein and rising pH; gastrin's main job is stimulating parietal cells |
| Intestinal | Acidic chyme reaches duodenum | Brief intestinal gastrin boost, then inhibition |

In the gastric phase, HCl release comes from combined acetylcholine (parasympathetic fibres), gastrin (G cells) and histamine (ECL cells, responding to gastrin). **High acidity (pH below 2) shuts gastrin off** by negative feedback; low acidity encourages it.

In the intestinal phase the **enterogastric reflex** blocks vagal drive from the medulla and activates sympathetic fibres, which tighten the pyloric sphincter and reduce secretion. Three duodenal hormones also reach the blood and inhibit gastric secretion:

| Hormone | Actions |
|---|---|
| Secretin | Pancreatic bicarbonate secretion; bile secretion by the liver |
| Cholecystokinin | More pancreatic enzymes; gallbladder contraction; relaxes the hepatopancreatic sphincter |
| VIP | Relaxes smooth muscle; stimulates water and electrolyte secretion in the intestine |

### Staying safe in a high-acid environment

Optimal enzyme function needs particular pH: mouth 6.4-7.3, stomach 1.5-4, duodenum to rectum 7-8.

| Attackers | Defenders |
|---|---|
| Acid from parietal cells | Mucin from goblet cells, forming a barrier and lubricant |
| Intrinsic factor accompanies acid | Tight junctions stop leakage of acid and entry of infection |
| Pepsinogen from chief cells, then pepsin | High cell turnover: stem cells in gastric pits renew the lining every 5-7 days |
| | Alkaline fluid from duodenal Brunner's glands |
| | Bile (bile salts and alkaline fluid) from hepatocytes |
| | Bicarbonate from the exocrine pancreas neutralises acid |

<Callout kind="warning" title="Intrinsic factor trap">
Intrinsic factor is made by the same parietal cells that make acid. Damage to these cells costs the patient acid and B12 absorption.
</Callout>

## Duodenum

- A C-shaped section of small intestine running from stomach to jejunum.
- The **hepatopancreatic sphincter** (sphincter of Oddi, duodenal ampulla, ampulla of Vater) controls bile and pancreatic juice entry at the major duodenal papilla.
- Histology: microvilli, crypts of Lieberkuhn, simple epithelium with goblet cells, and **Brunner's glands** producing strongly alkaline mucus.
- Neighbours: gallbladder with the cystic duct, hepatic ducts from the liver and the common hepatic duct, the bile duct, the pancreas and the pancreatic duct, and the jejunum beyond.

## Small intestine

| Part | Features |
|---|---|
| Duodenum | C-shaped |
| Jejunum | High surface area (plicae, villi, microvilli), nutrient absorption |
| Ileum | Fewer plicae and villi, fluid absorption |
| Ileocaecal valve | Empties into the caecum |

Microscopically: villous simple columnar epithelium with goblet cells and basal stem cells.

**Motility** is the migrating motor complex: pacemaker cells fire at 12-14 contractions a minute, a peristaltic wave begins in the duodenum and dies after 50-70 cm, new waves begin further on, and about 2 hours later the wave reaches the ileocaecal valve. The cycle repeats and sweeps out remnants and bacteria.

## Large intestine

**Functions:** absorbs water from indigestible residue, stores waste temporarily and eliminates semisolid faeces.

Distinctive structures:

| Structure | Detail |
|---|---|
| Teniae coli | Three ribbons of smooth muscle along the whole colon; they gather the wall into segments |
| Haustra | The resulting pouches; slow waste movement and help packing and compaction |
| Epiploic appendages | Fatty pouches of visceral peritoneum, function unknown |
| Transverse mesocolon | Peritoneal fold suspending the transverse colon |

Microscopically it has simple columnar epithelium with goblet cells. Motility consists of **mass movements**: the colon is mostly inactive, becomes motile when food arrives, and produces long, slow, powerful waves about 3 times a day over large areas to push contents towards the rectum.

## Rectum and anus

- Structures: levator ani muscle, anal canal, anus.
- **External anal sphincter:** voluntary. **Internal anal sphincter:** involuntary.
- **Defaecation reflex:** rectal stretch starts the reflex, the sigmoid colon and rectum contract and the internal sphincter relaxes. Pressure on the anal canal signals the brain, giving the urge.
