## What it is

**Hirschsprung's disease** (congenital aganglionic megacolon) is a **functional obstruction of the large bowel** caused by atonic bowel. Nothing physically blocks the lumen; the affected segment simply cannot move its contents on.

## Cause and pathogenesis

The condition is **congenital**. A section of large bowel has **no nerve (ganglion) cells** in its wall, so it is not innervated. Without nerve supply it cannot relax and propagate peristalsis, becomes paralysed and atonic, and causes obstruction.

## Morphology

- The aganglionic segment is usually short (under 30 cm) and lies in the distal large bowel. Classically it involves the rectum and sigmoid, always starting from the anus and extending upward.
- The atonic segment is **narrowed**.
- The bowel **proximal** to it is **dilated** because stool piles up behind the functional block.

## Clinical features

| Severity | When it presents | Features |
|---|---|---|
| Severe | Within 2-3 days of birth | Meconium not passed within 48 hours, then abdominal distension and vomiting |
| Mild | Late infancy | Abdominal distension, with constipation alternating with diarrhoea |

<Callout kind="pearl">
A newborn who has not passed meconium by 48 hours should prompt you to think of Hirschsprung's disease.
</Callout>

## Diagnosis

- **Barium enema** shows the narrow distal segment and dilated bowel above it.
- **Anorectal manometry** measures contractility of the anus and rectum.
- **Rectal biopsy** confirms the diagnosis by showing a lack of ganglion cells.

## Treatment

**Surgery**: resect the aganglionic bowel and join normal bowel to the anus (reanastomosis).

## Complications

- **Enterocolitis**, a serious bowel inflammation and infection behind the obstruction.
- Death if untreated.

<Callout kind="warning">
Fever, explosive diarrhoea and a swollen abdomen in a known or suspected case suggest enterocolitis and need urgent care.
</Callout>
