## What it is

**Inflammatory bowel disease (IBD)** covers two chronic conditions: **Crohn's disease (CD)** and **ulcerative colitis (UC)**. Both involve an exaggerated immune response against the gut's own flora, producing long-lasting inflammation.

<Anatomy figure="lower-gi" highlight={['ileum', 'rectum']} caption="Lower gi: ileum, rectum" />

## Causes and risk factors

- **Genetics and autoimmunity**: about 15% of patients have a family history.
- **Smoking**: a risk factor for Crohn's but **protective** for ulcerative colitis.
- **Western lifestyle** is associated with higher rates.
- **Appendicectomy** is protective for ulcerative colitis.
- **Breastfeeding** is protective for Crohn's.

## Features common to both

| Group | Features |
|---|---|
| Intestinal | Abdominal pain and severe cramps, vomiting and diarrhoea, rectal bleeding |
| Extra-intestinal | Primary sclerosing cholangitis, arthritis, pyoderma gangrenosum (skin), eye problems |

A common exam trap: primary biliary cirrhosis is sometimes written in place of primary sclerosing cholangitis. The recognised IBD association is **primary sclerosing cholangitis**, a bile duct disease, particularly with UC.

## Crohn's versus ulcerative colitis

| Feature | Crohn's disease | Ulcerative colitis |
|---|---|---|
| Where it starts | Terminal ileum | Rectum, spreading proximally |
| Extent in gut | Anywhere, mouth to anus | Colon only |
| Anus involved | Yes | No |
| Distribution | Patchy, with skip lesions | Continuous |
| Depth | Can be full thickness | Epithelial lining only |
| Stool | Porridge-like, sometimes steatorrhoea | Mucus-like with blood |
| Tenesmus | No | Yes |
| Fever | Yes | No |
| Fistulae | Yes | No |
| Weight loss | Yes | No |

Deep, transmural inflammation explains why Crohn's forms fistulae, strictures and perforation, while superficial disease in UC gives bloody mucus and tenesmus (rectal involvement). Malabsorption from small bowel disease explains weight loss and steatorrhoea in Crohn's.

<Callout kind="pearl">
Remember "Crohn's: Can be anywhere, Can be full thickness, Can fistulate." UC is continuous from the rectum, in the colon only.
</Callout>

## Diagnosis

**Colonoscopy with biopsy** confirms the diagnosis and shows the pattern of disease.

## Treatment

- **Corticosteroids** (for example prednisone) to control flares.
- **Immunosuppression and biologics**: infliximab, sulfasalazine, azathioprine and methotrexate (DMARD or chemotherapy-type drugs). Infliximab is an anti-TNF biologic; sulfasalazine is an aminosalicylate.
- **Surgery**: needed in about 75% of Crohn's and 20% of UC patients.
- **Yearly colonoscopy surveillance** for colon cancer.

## Complications

| Complication | Notes |
|---|---|
| Toxic megacolon | Common in UC, rare in Crohn's; dilation, stasis and gangrene |
| Bowel perforation | Typically Crohn's |
| Fistulae | Typically Crohn's |
| Anal fissures | Typically Crohn's |
| Colorectal cancer | Risk increased in both |

<Callout kind="warning" title="Toxic megacolon">
A grossly dilated colon with systemic illness risks perforation and gangrene and needs urgent assessment.
</Callout>
