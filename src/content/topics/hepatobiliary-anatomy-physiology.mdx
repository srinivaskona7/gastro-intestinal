## The liver at a glance

The liver is the body's largest gland, weighing about **1.5 kg**. It is wedge-shaped and has two surfaces, the **diaphragmatic** surface (facing up and forwards) and the **visceral** surface (facing the other organs). It has **four lobes**: right, left, quadrate and caudate (the "tail").

### What the liver does

| Area | Function |
|---|---|
| Bile | Makes bile, needed for fat digestion |
| Protein metabolism | Synthesis, storage and breakdown (including transamination) |
| Carbohydrate metabolism | Glucose to glycogen (**glycogenesis**); stored glycogen back to glucose (**glycogenolysis**) |
| Lipid metabolism | Makes VLDL and HDL, and handles lipid transport |
| Vitamins | Produces and stores vitamin A |
| Clotting | Makes heparin, an anti-clotting agent |
| Drugs | Detoxifies or activates them |
| Immunity | **Kupffer cells** are macrophages attached to the sinusoid endothelium |

### Peritoneal relations and blood supply

- The liver is covered by peritoneum, except for the **bare area** on the diaphragmatic surface.
- The **falciform ligament** attaches it to the anterior abdominal wall. Its free edge holds the **ligamentum teres** (round ligament), the remnant of the fetal umbilical vein.
- The **lesser omentum** runs from the liver to the lesser curvature of the stomach.
- **Dual blood supply:** hepatic artery 20%, portal vein 80%.
- All blood leaves the liver through the hepatic veins into the **inferior vena cava**.

### The portal triad

Three structures run together through the lesser omentum:

| Component | Origin | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Hepatic artery | Coeliac trunk | About 20% of supply; brings oxygen |
| Hepatic portal vein | Gastric, pancreatico-duodenal, splenic, superior and inferior mesenteric veins | About 80% of supply; carries nutrients absorbed along the whole gut to the liver |
| Common hepatic duct (bile duct) | Joined left and right hepatic ducts | Carries bile away |

### Nerves and lymph

- **Sympathetic** supply comes from the left and right sympathetic trunks; **parasympathetic** supply from branches of the vagus nerve, relayed through various ganglia and plexuses.
- Lymph drains to the coeliac, gastric, hepatic, cystic, pyloric and superior mesenteric nodes.

At each corner of a lobule lies a **portal triad** (a bile duct plus a branch of the portal vein and a branch of the hepatic artery). Hepatocytes are described in three zones by distance from it:

| 1 | Peripheral, closest to the portal triad |

## The biliary tree

**Inside the liver:** hepatocytes secrete bile into tiny **bile canaliculi**. These drain into larger bile ducts, which converge and eventually form the common duct.

**Outside the liver:**

1. Right and left hepatic ducts join as the **common hepatic duct**.
2. The **cystic duct** connects to the gallbladder.
3. The common hepatic duct plus the cystic duct form the **common bile duct**.
4. The pancreas drains through the **pancreatic duct**.
5. The common bile duct and pancreatic duct meet at the **hepatopancreatic ampulla**, which opens at the duodenal papilla.

## Gallbladder

The gallbladder **stores and concentrates bile**. It is pear-shaped and sits on the inferior surface of the liver, with its **fundus** resting on the transverse colon, then a body and a neck, all covered by visceral peritoneum. The cystic duct joins the common hepatic duct to form the bile duct, which reaches the duodenum at the papilla.

- Bile is greenish-yellow and **alkaline**, so it helps neutralise stomach acid in the duodenum.
- It contains cholesterol, bile salts (emulsifiers) and liver waste products including bilirubin.
- Hepatocytes make it; it is important for fat digestion.
- When bile is not flowing it backs up into the gallbladder, which removes water and **concentrates it about 5 times**.

### Bile composition and bile salts

Bile contains water, electrolytes, bile salts, phospholipids (both help lipid digestion and absorption), cholesterol and bilirubin.

Bile salts are made in the liver **from cholesterol**. The two primary ones are **cholic acid** and **chenodeoxycholic acid**. They are then **conjugated** with an amino acid, about **75% glycine and 25% taurine** (a cysteine derivative), which makes them water soluble.

### Functions of bile

- Essential for **digestion and absorption of fats** in the small intestine.
- A route for disposing of waste. Endogenous examples are calcium, cholesterol (kept soluble by bile salts), steroid hormones and bilirubin; exogenous examples are antibiotics and drug metabolites. These leave in the faeces, or are reabsorbed by the small intestine into blood and cleared by the kidneys into urine.
- Epithelium of the bile ducts adds a watery, bicarbonate-rich fluid.

<Callout kind="pearl" title="Gallstones">
Gallstones are precipitated cholesterol (in the gallbladder or cystic duct). They form when there is too much cholesterol or too few bile salts to keep it dissolved.
</Callout>

CCK (cholecystokinin, the "bile-sac moving hormone") is secreted by the duodenum. Bile finally enters the duodenum through the hepatopancreatic sphincter (**sphincter of Oddi**).

## Enterohepatic circulation

This is the recycling of bile salts ("intestine to liver"). About **90%** are reabsorbed in the **ileum** into venous blood, travel via the portal vein to the liver, are extracted by hepatocytes and re-secreted into the canaliculi. Each molecule is reused around **18-20 times**, so on average bile salts are turned over every 18-20 meals.

## Fat transport and the liver

- **Chylomicrons** ("fat shuttles") travel in lymph. In muscle and adipose tissue, **lipoprotein lipase** frees fatty acids from them, and the tissue re-makes triglycerides.
- The emptied **chylomicron remnants** go to the liver, which breaks them into glycerol, free fatty acids, cholesterol and phosphate.
- **Short-chain fatty acids** are absorbed directly into blood and carried by **albumin** (made by the liver), adding to the pool of breakdown products.

## Bilirubin metabolism

**Bilirubin** is a yellow waste product of heme breakdown, and also a powerful cellular antioxidant. Free (unconjugated) bilirubin is **toxic**; conjugated bilirubin is **water soluble and non-toxic**. About **75%** of body bilirubin comes from the haemoglobin of old red cells.

1. **Formation (mainly spleen):** old red cells are phagocytosed by macrophages of the spleen, bone marrow and liver. Haemoglobin splits into heme and globin; heme oxygenase turns heme into **biliverdin** and ferric iron (Fe3+); biliverdin reductase then makes **unconjugated bilirubin**.
2. **Transport:** unconjugated bilirubin is not water soluble, so it travels bound to **albumin** to the liver.
3. **Liver:** hepatocytes **conjugate** it by esterifying one or both carboxylic acid side chains with sugar residues (glucuronic acid, xylose or ribose), making it water soluble. It is excreted in bile into the gut.
4. **Gut:** colonic bacteria convert it to urobilinogen, then stercobilinogen and **stercobilin**, which colours faeces. Some is reabsorbed and excreted in urine as conjugated bilirubin and urobilinogen.

### Iron

The first step above releases iron. Fe3+ is carried in blood by **transferrin** to erythroblasts in bone marrow (for haemoglobin in new red cells) and to hepatocytes for storage. The liver stores iron as **ferritin** (a protein holding about 4500 iron ions per unit) or, when ferritin is full, as **haemosiderin** (a complex of iron with phosphate and hydroxide).

## Jaundice

**Jaundice** is yellow discolouration of skin, whites of the eyes and mucous membranes due to **hyperbilirubinaemia** (excess bilirubin in extracellular fluid).

| Type | Cause | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Pre-hepatic | Excess red cell breakdown (haemolysis) | Haemolytic anaemias |
| Hepatic | Damaged hepatocytes | Hepatitis, hepatotoxicity, alcoholic liver disease, hepatocyte necrosis |
| Post-hepatic (obstructive) | Blocked bile drainage | Commonly gallstones |

### Liver tests

| Group | Tests | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Hepatocellular injury or inflammation | AST, ALT | Cell damage |
| Biliary tree pathology (intra- or extra-hepatic) | Alkaline phosphatase, gamma-GT | Cholestasis |
| Synthetic function | Serum albumin, blood urea, clotting factors | Reflect how well the liver is making proteins |

## Drug metabolism (biotransformation)

**First-pass metabolism:** oral drugs pass through the intestine and liver before reaching their site of action. Some are metabolised in the liver, some in the gut, so the dose given is not the amount available to the body (**bioavailability**).

Liver enzymes can turn a drug into active, inactive, toxic or non-toxic metabolites:

| Process | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Bioactivation | Pro-drugs are given inactive and activated by the body; some active drugs also have active metabolites (codeine, a cough suppressant, becomes morphine, an analgesic) |
| Bioinactivation | Drug action ends through inactive metabolites |
| Toxification | Metabolite is far more toxic than the parent drug |
| Detoxification | Toxin action ends via a less effective metabolite |
| Pre-elimination | Most drugs are lipophilic and would be reabsorbed by the kidneys, so they must become hydrophilic for urinary excretion |

### Phase I and Phase II

| | Phase I | Phase II (conjugation) |
|---|---|---|
| What happens | A functional group is exposed or added (new groups, interchange, or unmasking existing ones) | Drug or metabolite is joined to an endogenous polar compound so it dissolves in water for renal excretion |
| Main types | Oxidation is the most common | **Glucuronide conjugation** (most common), glutathione, amino acid, sulphate, acetylation, methylation |
| Enzymes | Oxidation by the **cytochrome P450 mono-oxygenase** family | Transferases |

P450 enzymes have other jobs. In synthesis: cholesterol to bile acid, hydroxylation of steroids and vitamin D, alkanes to fatty acids, fatty acids to eicosanoids. In catabolism: breakdown of fatty acids, steroids and eicosanoids.

### Factors that alter biotransformation

| Factor | Effect |
|---|---|
| Other drugs (including herbal remedies) | Change enzyme expression or activity, or compete for enzymes and pathways |
| Genetics | Variable P450 expression: CYP2D6 is absent in about 7% of Caucasians (no response) and hyperactive in about 30% of East Africans (same dose can be toxic) |
| Disease | Liver, kidney, heart or vascular disease; viral infections change enzyme activity; bacterial toxins alter drug metabolism |
| Hormones | Oestrogen affects enzyme activity |
| Age and sex | Enzyme expression changes with age; sex differences reflect hormones |
| Diet | Certain foods change enzyme activity |

## Pancreas

- **Anatomy:** elongated, horizontal and **retroperitoneal**. The head is cupped by the duodenum; the tail touches the spleen.
- **Blood supply:** pancreatico-duodenal artery; veins drain via the splenic vein branches into the portal vein.
- **Lymph:** coeliac, pyloric and superior mesenteric nodes. **Nerves:** sympathetic and parasympathetic fibres via ganglia and plexuses.

| Part | Share | Product |
|---|---|---|
| Exocrine (acinar cells and ducts) | 85% | Pancreatic juice |
| Endocrine (islets of Langerhans) | 15% | Four hormones |

**Exocrine juice:** enzymes are stored as inactive **zymogens** in membrane-bound vesicles, released into the pancreatic ducts, activated, and drain through the main pancreatic duct into the duodenum.

| Component | Digests |
|---|---|
| Amylase | Carbohydrates |
| Lipase | Fats |
| Chymotrypsin, aminopeptidase, elastase | Proteins (elastase especially elastin) |
| Nuclease | Nucleic acids |
| Bicarbonate | Neutralises acid |

**Islet cells:** beta cells make **insulin** (lowers blood glucose), alpha cells make **glucagon** (raises it), delta cells make **somatostatin** (inhibits release of many hormones), and PP cells make **pancreatic polypeptide**, involved in satiety.
