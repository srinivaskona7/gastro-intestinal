## What it is

**Appendicitis** is acute inflammation of the vermiform appendix. The cause is often idiopathic (not identified), but the usual mechanism is blockage of the lumen.

<Anatomy figure="lower-gi" highlight={['appendix', 'mcburneys-point']} caption="Lower gi: appendix, mcburneys point" />

## Pathogenesis

A **faecolith** (hard faecal pellet) obstructs the appendix. Mucus builds up and the appendix cannot drain (stasis), bacteria multiply (infection), the wall loses its blood supply (gangrene), and finally it **perforates**.

## Morphology

The appendix is enlarged and inflamed.

## Clinical features

The classic story: pain begins **around the umbilicus** (visceral pain from the midgut) and then **moves to the right iliac fossa (RIF)** when the inflamed appendix irritates the parietal peritoneum.

Other symptoms: nausea, vomiting, anorexia and occasionally diarrhoea. On examination there is RIF pain, tenderness and guarding.

| Sign | How it is elicited |
|---|---|
| Rovsing's | Pressing the left iliac fossa causes more pain in the RIF |
| Psoas | Pain on extending, flexing or rotating the hip (irritated psoas muscle, typically with a retrocaecal appendix) |
| McBurney's | Deep tenderness at McBurney's point in the RIF |
| Obturator | Pain on rotation of the flexed hip (pelvic appendix) |
| Referred rebound | Rebound tenderness felt in the LIF, though worst in the RIF |

<Callout kind="pearl">
Rovsing's sign: press on the left, hurt on the right. Psoas and obturator signs reflect the appendix's position relative to those muscles.
</Callout>

## Diagnosis

- **Ultrasound or CT** shows an enlarged, thickened appendix.
- **FBC**: raised white cells with neutrophilia.
- Raised **ESR and CRP**.

## Treatment

1. **Haemodynamic stabilisation** with fluids, plus group and hold (save a blood sample for cross-match).
2. **Prophylactic antibiotics**: ampicillin, gentamicin and metronidazole.
3. **Appendicectomy**, open or laparoscopic.

## Complications

- Perforation
- Sepsis

<Callout kind="warning" title="Perforation">
Sudden relief of pain followed by fever, rigidity and tachycardia can mean perforation and peritonitis. Surgery should not be delayed.
</Callout>
