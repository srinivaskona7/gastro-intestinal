## What it is

GI bleeding is common: most people experience some form of it in their lifetime. It may be **trivial or life-threatening**, **painful or painless**, and the underlying cause may or may not be serious.

## Terms to know

| Term | Meaning | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Haematemesis | Vomiting blood, fresh or dark (denatured) | Can be very serious; usually implies a stomach source |
| Haematochezia | Passage of bloody stools (rectal bleeding) | Major bleeding implies an upper GI source; minor implies a lower GI source |
| Melaena | Black, tar-like stool | Usually an upper GI source |
| Occult bleeding | Hidden blood loss | Important cause of chronic anaemia; detected by faecal occult blood test |

<Callout kind="warning" title="Haematemesis first priority">
Get **large-bore IV access** and **cross-match blood** straight away. Bleeding from the stomach can become fatal very quickly.
</Callout>

## Major versus minor rectal bleeding

The split is clinical. **Major** bleeding means the patient needs resuscitation: tachycardia, hypotension, pallor, dyspnoea or altered level of consciousness. **Minor** bleeding means the patient is haemodynamically stable.

### Commonest causes

| Bleed | Cause | Share |
|---|---|---|
| Major, upper GI | Peptic ulcer, duodenal ulcer, oesophageal varices | Commonest upper causes |
| Major, lower GI | Diverticulosis | 50% |
| | Angiodysplasia | 35% |
| | Colorectal cancer | 15% |
| Minor, lower GI | Haemorrhoids | 54% |
| | Fissure | 18% |
| | Fistulae | 7% |
| | Cancer or polyp | 6% |

<Callout kind="pearl">
Blood colour gives clues. **Frank red blood** suggests massive bleeding or a more distal origin. **Blackened blood** suggests slower bleeding or a stomach or oesophageal origin.
</Callout>

## Investigations and management

- **FBC**: haemoglobin, reticulocytes, platelets and MCV (a low MCV points to chronic loss).
- **LFTs**: look for alcoholic liver disease (varices).
- **Coagulation studies** and **EUCs**.
- **PPI** if peptic ulcer disease is suspected.
- **Group and hold, cross-match** and **IV fluids** if the bleed is severe.
- **Endoscopy or colonoscopy** is the definitive investigation.
