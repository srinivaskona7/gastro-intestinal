## What it is

The liver can host two very different kinds of cancer.

- **Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC)** is the **primary** liver tumour. It grows from the liver's own cells, the hepatocytes.
- **Liver metastases** are **secondary** tumours. Cancer from somewhere else has seeded into the liver. They are the most common malignancy found in the liver.

## Hepatocellular carcinoma

### Causes / risk factors

Risk is extreme in two settings:

- **Chronic hepatitis B and C infection**
- **Cirrhosis** of any cause

### Pathogenesis

Cirrhosis comes first. Long-standing damage (alcohol, viral hepatitis, haemochromatosis) causes scarring and constant cell regeneration, and in that setting a malignant clone eventually emerges. So HCC is essentially a late complication of chronic liver disease.

### Morphology

The tumour may be a **single mass or several**. Under the microscope the cells still look like hepatocytes.

### Clinical features

| Type | Findings |
|---|---|
| General features | Fever, night sweats, anorexia, weight loss |
| Local features | Right upper quadrant pain, jaundice, itch |
| Signs of the underlying liver disease | Hepatic encephalopathy with flapping tremor, a small nodular liver, ascites |

<Callout kind="warning" title="Think HCC in any cirrhotic who deteriorates">
The signs above are those of cirrhosis itself. When a known cirrhotic develops them afresh, or gets worse, suspect that an HCC has developed.
</Callout>

### Investigations

| Test | What it shows |
|---|---|
| Serum alpha-fetoprotein | Raised |
| LFTs | Generally deranged, with everything raised |
| FBC | Look at the MCV, for infection and for haemolysis (reticulocytes) |
| Liver ultrasound | A homogeneous mass |
| FNA and biopsy | Gives the tumour grade |
| CT or MRI | Staging |

### Management

**Surgery** is the treatment. Chemotherapy and radiotherapy are unhelpful.

### Complications and prognosis

Tumour cells escape via the **hepatic veins, portal veins and lymphatics**, and most often lodge in **bone and lung**. The prognosis is **poor**, with an average survival of about six months from diagnosis.

## Liver metastases

### Causes and pathogenesis

A cancer at a distant site spreads through the blood or by direct seeding and lodges in the liver. The commonest primaries are **breast, colorectal, lung and melanoma**. The liver receives portal blood from the bowel and a rich arterial supply, so it is a frequent landing site.

### Morphology

Macroscopically there are **multiple, clearly demarcated masses**.

### Clinical features

Features overlap with HCC: fever, night sweats, anorexia, weight loss, RUQ pain, jaundice and itch. Advanced disease adds **cachexia**, nausea and hepatic encephalopathy.

### Investigations and management

- **CT** is used mainly for prognosis.
- **LFTs** are generally raised.
- If the primary is unknown, tissue sampling is worthwhile because the tumour may turn out to be chemoresponsive.
- Management is **palliative**: chemotherapy and analgesia.

### Comparing the two

| | HCC | Metastases |
|---|---|---|
| Origin | Hepatocytes | Elsewhere (breast, colorectal, lung, melanoma) |
| Background | Cirrhosis, hepatitis B and C | Any known or hidden primary |
| Appearance | Single or multiple, hepatocyte-like cells | Multiple, well-demarcated |
| Marker | Raised alpha-fetoprotein | Not specific |
| Treatment | Surgery | Palliative chemotherapy and analgesia |

<Callout kind="pearl">
Primary HCC is the classic liver cancer in exams, but statistically a liver tumour in a non-cirrhotic patient is far more likely to be a metastasis.
</Callout>
