## Dysphagia: a symptom, not a disease

**Dysphagia** means difficulty swallowing. It tells you something is wrong but not what, so the task is to find the cause. Do not confuse it with **odynophagia**, which is *painful* swallowing.

The problem can sit at four sites: **oropharyngeal**, **oesophageal**, **gastro-oesophageal** or **para-oesophageal** (outside the oesophagus, pressing on it).

### Pattern points to cause

| What is hard to swallow | Likely mechanism | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Solids only | Mechanical obstruction | Strictures, tumours |
| Solids and liquids | Motility disorder | Achalasia, neural (vagus nerve) problems |
| Liquids only | Pharyngeal disorder | Globus pharyngeus |

<Callout kind="warning" title="Common confusion">
Globus pharyngeus is classically a sensation of a lump in the throat without true swallowing difficulty. A liquids-only pattern is a teaching shortcut for pharyngeal causes; in practice also consider neurological causes of oropharyngeal dysphagia.
</Callout>

## Achalasia

### Causes and pathogenesis

The cause is **unknown**. Proposed mechanisms are autoimmune, neurodegenerative and viral. Damage is often attributed to **vagus nerve dysfunction**, leading to two problems: **absent oesophageal peristalsis (aperistalsis)** and **impaired relaxation of the lower oesophageal sphincter**. Food therefore cannot be pushed along and the sphincter stays tight. (Textbooks locate the underlying defect in loss of inhibitory neurons of the oesophageal wall nerve plexus, with the same functional result.)

### Clinical features

- **Dysphagia** with a long history, intermittent, affecting **both liquids and solids from the onset**. This early liquid involvement is what separates it from a stricture or tumour.
- **Reflux**, particularly at night, which can lead to **aspiration pneumonia**.
- **Chest pain** from oesophageal spasm.

### Investigations and management

| Step | Detail |
|---|---|
| Investigations | Barium swallow; endoscopy |
| Management | Palliative treatment: balloon dilation, or surgery |

Treatment does not restore peristalsis; it relieves the outflow obstruction at the sphincter.
