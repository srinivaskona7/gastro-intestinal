## What it is

An **anal fistula** is an abnormal channel connecting the inside of the anal canal to the skin around the anus. Most are described as **cryptogenic**, meaning they arise from the **anal crypts** with no other obvious cause: infection of a crypt gland forms an abscess that then drains and leaves a persistent tract.

<Callout kind="warning" title="Common confusion">
Raised abdominal pressure and incompetent valves explain haemorrhoids, not fistulae. Cryptogenic crypt origin is the standard explanation for fistulae.
</Callout>

## Types

Fistulae are classified by their course relative to the anal sphincter muscles.

| Type | Note |
|---|---|
| Intersphincteric | **Commonest** route, between the internal and external sphincters |
| Transsphincteric | Crosses the external sphincter |
| Suprasphincteric | Loops up and over the sphincter complex |
| Extrasphincteric | Runs outside the sphincters |
| Submucosal | Superficial, beneath the mucosa |

## Clinical features

- **Pain**
- **Discharge** from the opening
- **Fever**
- **Incontinence** complaints (usually soiling or leakage rather than true sphincter failure)

## Diagnosis and management

- **Diagnosis** is by clinical examination.
- **Management** is surgical: **fistulotomy**, laying the tract open so it heals from the base.

## Complications

The main complication is **anorectal sepsis**, from persistent or undrained infection.

<Callout kind="pearl">
Fistula means pain, discharge and fever; the commonest route is intersphincteric. Fistulae account for about 7% of minor rectal bleeding.
</Callout>
