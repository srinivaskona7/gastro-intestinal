## What it is

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Diverticulosis** | Presence of diverticula (pouches) in the bowel wall |
| **Diverticulitis** | Inflammation of those diverticula |

A diverticulum is a pocket of mucosa pushed out through the muscular wall, typically in the colon and most often on the **left** side (sigmoid).

<Anatomy figure="lower-gi" highlight={['sigmoid-colon']} caption="Lower gi: sigmoid colon" />

## Causes and risk factors

- Straining at stool
- Chronic constipation
- Increasing age
- Congenital forms, such as **Meckel's diverticulum** (a true congenital pouch in the ileum)

## Pathogenesis

**Diverticulosis**: a weakness in the intestinal or colonic wall combines with raised intraluminal pressure (from straining or constipation). The mucosa herniates through the weak point and forms a pouch.

**Diverticulitis**: faeces block the neck of the diverticulum, contents stagnate, bacteria overgrow, and inflammation follows. That inflammation can lead to complications.

## Morphology

Pouches or pockets in the intestinal wall, typically in the left iliac fossa (LIF) region of the colon.

## Clinical features

| Situation | Features |
|---|---|
| Diverticulosis | 95% asymptomatic; rectal bleeding common |
| Symptomatic disease | Intermittent LIF pain and erratic bowel habit |
| Diverticulitis | Severe LIF pain, fever, tachycardia (like appendicitis, but on the left) |
| Perforation | Peritonitis and sepsis |

<Callout kind="pearl">
Left-sided appendicitis-like pain with fever in an older, constipated patient is diverticulitis until proven otherwise.
</Callout>

## Diagnosis

- **Colonoscopy** to see the pouches (avoided during acute inflammation because of perforation risk, with CT often used instead).
- **FBC** to look for raised inflammatory markers and bleeding.

## Treatment

- **High fibre diet** for prevention and symptoms.
- **Non-perforated diverticulitis**: antibiotics, here ampicillin, gentamicin and metronidazole.
- **Perforated diverticulitis**: the same antibiotics plus **surgery**.

## Complications

- **Rectal bleeding** (the commonest)
- **Perforation** leading to sepsis
- Generalised peritonitis
- Abscess
- Fistulae into adjacent organs

<Callout kind="warning" title="Perforation">
Rigid abdomen, high fever and shock suggest perforation with peritonitis, which needs resuscitation, antibiotics and surgery.
</Callout>
