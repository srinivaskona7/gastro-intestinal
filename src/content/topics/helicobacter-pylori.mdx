## What it is

**Helicobacter pylori (HP)** is a Gram-negative, spiral-shaped bacterium that lives in the stomach lining. It is found in about **75% of patients with peptic ulcer disease**.

| Feature | Detail |
|---|---|
| Transmission | Oral-oral, faecal-oral, and vertical (mother to child) |
| Where it colonises | First part of the duodenum, then antrum, then gastro-oesophageal junction |
| Urease | Produces ammonia to neutralise stomach acid |
| Oxygen need | Microaerophilic (dislikes high oxygen) |
| Growth | Metabolically fastidious, so it grows slowly |
| Carcinogen status | Class I, a definite carcinogen |

It is carcinogenic because chronic inflammation and long exposure to reactive oxygen species cause **metaplasia and dysplasia** of gastric cells, which can progress to adenocarcinoma.

## Pathogenesis

Where the bacteria settle decides which ulcer develops.

<Mermaid
  caption="Two routes to ulcer"
  chart={`flowchart TD
  A["H pylori"] --> B["Colonises corpus"]
  A --> C["Colonises antrum"]
  B --> D["Gastritis and epithelial damage"]
  D --> E["Acid exposure"]
  E --> F["Gastric ulcer"]
  C --> G["Antral gastritis, fewer D cells and less somatostatin"]
  G --> H["Less inhibition of G cells, more gastrin"]
  H --> I["More histamine mediated acid from parietal cells"]
  I --> J["Duodenal acidity, gastric metaplasia, HP colonises, duodenitis"]
  J --> K["Duodenal ulcer"]`}
/>

**Gastric ulcer route**: HP colonises the corpus, causing gastritis and damaging the epithelium, which is then exposed to acid. Loss of antral D cells (and somatostatin) also reduces inhibition of G cells, so gastrin and histamine-mediated acid secretion from parietal cells rise and potentiate the ulcer.

**Duodenal ulcer route**: raised stomach acid and HP colonising the antrum cause gastritis. Fewer D cells and less somatostatin mean less G cell inhibition, so more gastrin and more histamine-driven acid. The duodenum becomes more acidic, develops metaplasia, is colonised by HP, becomes inflamed (duodenitis) and ulcerates.

## Adaptations and pathogenicity factors

| Factor | Role |
|---|---|
| Carbohydrate metabolism | Can use glucose; has pyruvate and fumarate pathways |
| Urease | Hydrolyses urea to ammonia and bicarbonate, neutralising acid |
| Motility | Flagellin gives powerful swimming |
| Adhesins | Stick to the epithelium, where pH is less acidic |
| Type IV secretion system | A molecular syringe injecting cytotoxins such as **CagA** |
| CagA effects | Interferes with epithelial tight junctions (leaky barrier), induces COX-2 and prostaglandins (inflammation), triggers inflammatory cytokines |

## Clinical features

- Worldwide distribution, highest in less developed countries and linked to low socioeconomic status.
- Infection rates are high in the elderly.
- If untreated it becomes chronic, so the person is infected for life.
- HP-related diseases include gastritis, peptic ulcer, gastric adenocarcinoma and MALT lymphoma.

## Diagnosis

- **Gastric biopsy**: histology and culture.
- **Serology**: detects antibodies against HP antigens (gel electrophoresis).
- **Carbon-13 urea breath test**: the patient drinks urea labelled with carbon-13. HP urease splits it and the labelled carbon appears in CO2, which is detected in exhaled breath. A positive result proves urease, and therefore HP, is present.

## Treatment

- **Combined triple antibiotics plus a PPI**. The PPI lowers harmful acid; the antibiotics kill the bacteria.
- A **bismuth chelate** preparation is toxic to HP, blocks adherence to mucosa and inhibits its proteolytic enzymes.
- Cure rate is below 80% and antibiotic resistance is rising, which drives the push for a vaccine.

<Callout kind="warning" title="Resistance">
Eradication fails in a sizeable minority and resistance is increasing. Confirm cure rather than assuming it.
</Callout>

## Vaccine progress

- Works in some animal models, but results in humans are poor.
- More than 100 antigens identified.
- Protection does not depend on antibodies, so the mechanism is unclear; CD4 T cells are implicated.
- Unknown whether a safe vaccine is feasible; post-immunisation gastritis is a problem and efficacy evidence in humans is minimal.

<Callout kind="pearl">
Urease is the key trick: it protects the bacterium from acid and is also the basis of the breath test.
</Callout>
