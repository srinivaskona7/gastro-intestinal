## What optimal nutrition means

Nutrition is **optimal when the body's requirements equal its supply**. Food covers two kinds of requirement:

- **Maintenance:** the basic needs, reflected by the **basal metabolic rate (BMR)**, the energy spent just to stay alive.
- **Performance or activity:** extra energy used for movement, thinking and digestion. Food is needed not only for nutrients but also to give the GI tract its own "exercise".

### Routes of giving nutrition

| Route | How |
|---|---|
| Self | Eating |
| Enteral | Nasogastric tube, delivering straight to stomach or jejunum |
| Parenteral | Catheter into the venous system (for example the subclavian vein) |

## Energy

**BMR** (also called resting metabolic rate) is the energy used when lying down, completely at rest (mentally and physically), in a thermo-neutral environment and in the **post-absorptive state** (nothing being digested, since digestion costs energy).

Food energy is measured in **joules** (usually kJ or MJ). One joule is the energy needed to move 1 kg through 1 m using 1 newton of force. A rate of energy use is in **watts** (1 W = 1 J per second). The older unit, the calorie, equals 4.184 J.

### From food to usable energy

The body harvests food energy efficiently, but some is lost as faeces, urine, methane and heat.

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Gross energy (GE) | Total energy content of the food |
| Digestible energy (DE) | GE minus energy lost in faeces |
| Metabolisable energy (ME) | GE minus faeces, urine and methane (CH4) |
| Net energy (NE) | ME minus heat lost to surroundings; left for maintenance, movement and storage |

### Energy content of macronutrients

Food energy is quoted as metabolisable energy per unit of **dry matter (DM)**, in kJ/g or MJ/kg.

| Nutrient | Energy | Share of diet |
|---|---|---|
| Carbohydrate | 16 kJ/g DM | 55-60% |
| Protein | 17 kJ/g DM (about 1.5 times carbohydrate) | 15% |
| Fat | 37 kJ/g DM (about 2.5 times carbohydrate) | 30% |

<Callout kind="pearl">
Protein's gross value is about 23 kJ/g, but roughly 6 kJ/g is lost in metabolism (nitrogen waste), leaving the 17 kJ/g figure.
</Callout>

## Why each nutrient class matters

- **Carbohydrates:** mainly energy. Dietary fibre adds faecal bulk, supports regular bowel movements and keeps colonocytes healthy.
- **Proteins:** 9 of the 20 amino acids are **essential** (the body cannot make them): histidine, isoleucine, leucine, lysine, methionine, phenylalanine, threonine, tryptophan and valine. Methionine is also the precursor of cysteine, and phenylalanine of tyrosine.
- **Fats:** cannot be removed completely from the diet. Some fatty acids are essential: linoleic acid (plant oils, omega-6) which gives arachidonic acid, a prostaglandin precursor; and linolenic acid (plant oils, omega-3) which gives EPA and DHA. Fats also carry the fat-soluble vitamins.
- **Vitamins:** fat-soluble A, D, E and K; important water-soluble ones include B12 and biotin (both in fatty acid metabolism) and vitamin C.

## Glycaemic index

The **glycaemic index (GI)** is how strongly a food raises blood glucose over 2 hours, with pure glucose as the reference (100% of the area under the curve). Foods with more complex carbohydrate digest more slowly and have a lower GI.

| | High GI | Low GI |
|---|---|---|
| Energy release | Quick and short | Slow and sustained |
| Carbohydrate type | Simpler | More complex |

## What can go wrong

Advanced malnutrition (obesity, anorexia) is easy to spot because complications show, so the aim is to **diagnose early**, to give support and advice before complications.

In secondary deficiency the diet is fine, but the body cannot absorb or use what is there.

## Assessing nutritional status

### Dietary history

Food diary, 24-hour recall, diet history and food frequency questionnaire.

### Biochemical tests

Blood and urine tests may reflect nutritional status directly, or show things that will affect it later (electrolyte balance, acid-base balance, organ function). **Limitations:** results need interpretation, hydration alters them, several tests over time are needed, and levels can be unrelated to nutrition.

**Protein-energy malnutrition (PEM):**

| Marker | Notes |
|---|---|
| Serum proteins: albumin, transferrin, transthyretin (binds thyroid hormone), retinol-binding protein (binds vitamin A), clotting factors | Reflect liver function, intake, distribution (cells versus blood) and utilisation. The shorter the half-life, the more sensitive the test |
| Nitrogen balance | Protein intake minus nitrogen lost in faeces and urine |
| Urinary creatinine | Proportional to muscle mass |
| Urinary or blood urea | Rises with increased proteolysis |

**Fat:** lipoprotein levels (triglyceride, phospholipid, cholesterol) - chylomicrons (from intestine), VLDL (high triglyceride; liver to periphery), LDL (high cholesterol), HDL (high protein). Useful for cardiovascular risk.

**Glucose:** blood glucose is only a snapshot, so many readings are needed; urinary glucose suggests blood glucose is high. This sorts patients into normal, impaired or diabetic, followed by a **glucose tolerance test** and/or **HbA1c**. Sustained high glucose glycosylates haemoglobin, so HbA1c reflects about the last **120 days**, the lifespan of a red cell.

**Minerals and vitamins:**

| Nutrient | Assessment |
|---|---|
| Iron | Haemoglobin, haematocrit (percentage of blood that is red cells), serum ferritin (stores), transferrin saturation (serum iron over total iron-binding capacity) |
| Folate | Serum folate; mean corpuscular volume (average red cell size) |
| Vitamin B12 | Serum B12; **Schilling test** for malabsorption |

In the **Schilling test** the person is first saturated with an injection of B12, then given oral radio-labelled B12, and the labelled B12 in urine is measured. Malabsorption may be due to lack of intrinsic factor.

### Physical examination

Clinical assessment for signs of deficiency or excess.

### Anthropometry (body measurements)

- **Growth charts:** growth (length, weight, height) is plotted in percentiles. The concern is a child who jumps between percentile lines; a boy born on the 25th percentile is expected to stay near it.
- **Skinfold (pinch) tests** estimate body fat.
- **BMI** = mass (kg) divided by height (m) squared.

| BMI | Category |
|---|---|
| Below 18.5 | Underweight |
| 18.5-25 | Acceptable |
| 25-30 | Overweight |
| 30 or more | Obese |

- **Waist:hip ratio:** recommended about 0.8 for men and 0.9 for women.
