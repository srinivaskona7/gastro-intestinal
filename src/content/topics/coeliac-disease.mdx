## What it is

**Coeliac disease** is a chronic inflammation of the **proximal small bowel** set off by **gluten**. It is sometimes loosely called an allergic inflammation, but strictly it is an immune-mediated (autoimmune-type) reaction, which is why it is tested for with antibodies and does not behave like a simple food allergy.

## Cause

People who are immunologically sensitive react to **gluten and related prolamins** found in:

- wheat
- barley
- rye

## Pathogenesis and morphology

Repeated exposure causes chronic inflammation of the lining. The villi, which are the finger-like folds that give the bowel its absorptive surface, are lost. The crypts between them compensate by growing.

| Mucosal surface | Folded | Flattened |
| Crypts | Normal depth | Hyperplastic |
| Site | Whole small bowel | Mainly proximal |

## Clinical features

**Epidemiology:** it can appear at **any age**, with peaks in **infancy** and in the **fifties**. It is **commoner in women** than men.

Symptoms and signs:

- Fatigue and malaise
- Diarrhoea, steatorrhoea (fatty stools) or even constipation
- Abdominal pain, discomfort and bloating
- Weight loss
- Angular stomatitis and mouth ulcers (from iron, folate and vitamin deficiency)

## Investigations

- **Duodenal biopsy** showing villous atrophy is the key test.
- **Immunological testing** for anti-transglutaminase antibodies.

<Callout kind="pearl">
Test the serology while the patient is still eating gluten. A gluten-free diet started beforehand can make antibodies and biopsy falsely normal.
</Callout>

## Management

- **Gluten-free diet for life** is the treatment.
- **Supplements** for deficiencies: vitamins, iron, folate, calcium and vitamin D.
- Enzyme replacement therapy is a possible future option.

## Complications

- **Ulcerative jejunitis**: fever, abdominal pain, perforation and bleeding.
- Higher risk of **GI malignancies**.
- Higher risk of **inflammatory bowel disease**.
- **Osteomalacia** that can progress to **osteoporosis**, from poor calcium and vitamin D absorption.

<Callout kind="warning" title="New symptoms in known coeliac disease">
Fever, abdominal pain or bleeding in someone already on a gluten-free diet raises concern for ulcerative jejunitis or malignancy and needs prompt assessment.
</Callout>
